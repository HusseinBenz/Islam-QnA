The question makes sense whenever we are talking about something that came into existence. In Islamic theology, God is eternal. The Qur’an calls him “the First,” and a prayer attributed to Muhammad explains that nothing precedes him.

The book uses two steps. **Something that begins has a cause.** That's a narrower premise than saying that everything that exists must have a creator. **A chain of dependent causes still raises a question about what ultimately accounts for the chain.** Al-Sayyid argues that extending the chain forever supplies no independent source. The philosophical question is whether an uncreated foundation is needed and whether the evidence points to God.

A hadith also addresses the experience of the question repeating as an intrusive thought. Its advice is to seek refuge in God and disengage from the loop. Ibn Taymiyya reads the report as advice to recognize when a chain of questions has reached the kind of explanation it was seeking.
