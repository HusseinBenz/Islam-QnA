Much of what later Muslims learned about Muhammad came through people who knew him. That makes the first generation's role in transmission central to the Sunni case.

Al-Juwayni connects the Companions' standing with their work in transmitting the religion. Al-Ghazali points to the praise given to them in scripture and reports, and to the sacrifices many made in migration and in sustaining the community. Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr, al-ʿAlaʾi and Ibn Taymiyya describe the presumption of integrity as a settled Sunni position.

In hadith scholarship that presumption concerns their honesty as a generation. Sunni scholars still distinguish it from infallibility and examine whether the chain that attributes a particular report to a Companion is sound. Other Islamic traditions have applied different assessments to individual Companions.
