Imagine reading only the first half of the report in Sahih Muslim. You would hear an instruction against writing down material other than the Quran. Read further and you find permission to relate what Muhammad said. The two instructions concern different ways of preserving his words.

Other reports add context. Muhammad approved putting his sermon in writing for Abu Shah and allowed ʿAbd Allah ibn ʿAmr to record what he heard. Some scholars took that permission to supersede the restriction. Ibn Hajar reports that al-Bukhari preferred to attribute the restrictive statement to the Companion Abu Saʿid al-Khudri.

A reader who cites only the restriction is relying on a transmitted report while omitting evidence transmitted in the same way. Looking at the full set of reports helps explain why a dispute over early writing practices does not by itself settle the authority of the Sunnah.
