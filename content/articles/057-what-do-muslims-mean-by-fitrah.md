The word *fitrah* comes from a root meaning "to originate" or "to bring into being." It refers to the nature with which God creates human beings. The key verse is:

> *"So direct your face toward the religion, inclining to truth, the fitrah of God upon which He created people. There is no changing God's creation. That is the upright religion, but most people do not know."* (Quran 30:30)

The Prophet ﷺ explained it with an image: *"Every child is born upon the fitrah. Then his parents make him a Jew, a Christian or a Magian, just as an animal gives birth to a whole animal. Do you see any of them born mutilated?"* (Sahih al-Bukhari 1385). In a hadith in which the Prophet reports God's words, God says: *"I created My servants all inclining to the truth (hunafa'). Then devils came to them and led them away from their religion"* (Sahih Muslim 2865).

## What the fitrah contains

Ibn Taymiyya returns to the fitrah again and again, especially in *Dar' Ta'arud al-'Aql wa-l-Naql* and in his fatwas. His view is that the fitrah is not a set of ready-made doctrines. It is a **readiness** to recognise the truth and love it when nothing obstructs it, as an infant is ready for milk. Three aspects stand out.

**1. Basic reasoning.** We know without being taught that a part is smaller than the whole, that something cannot both be and not be in the same respect, and that when something begins to exist we look for its cause. These first principles are the tools of all thinking, including thinking about a Creator. The Qur'an appeals to them directly: *"Is there any doubt about God, Creator of the heavens and the earth?"* (14:10).

**2. Dependence and worship.** Across cultures and centuries, people have worshipped, prayed and sought help from beyond themselves, especially in danger. The Qur'an notes how this surfaces at sea: *"When they board a ship, they call upon God, sincere to Him in religion. But when He delivers them to land, at once they associate others with Him"* (29:65). The prophets, on this view, did not introduce an alien idea. They reminded people of what they already sensed, and called them to worship God alone.

**3. Instinct and conscience.** A newborn knows how to feed. Parents love their children without being argued into it. Almost everyone senses that cruelty for its own sake is wrong. The Qur'an speaks of the soul *"and He inspired it with its wickedness and its righteousness"* (91:8). The Egyptian thinker Abdelwahab Elmessiri, who moved from materialism to belief, used to ask whether a strictly material account could really exhaust the meaning of a mother's love for her child.

## Covered, not erased

The hadith says parents and surroundings *shape* the child. They do not change its nature. *"There is no changing God's creation"* (30:30). Upbringing, culture, habits and arguments can cover the fitrah, distort how it is expressed, or make a person suspicious of it. They do not remove it. That is why Muslims see turning to Islam less as becoming something foreign than as **returning** to something original.

## What the argument is, and is not

The fitrah is not offered as a laboratory proof. It is a theological and philosophical reading of familiar experiences: the force of first principles, the near-universal turn to the transcendent, and the reality of conscience. These make sense if we were made by a wise and good Creator. People can and do disagree about how universal such a disposition is, and about what best explains it. The Muslim claim is that belief in God explains these features of human life more naturally than the alternatives. See also [creation as evidence for God](article.html?id=58).
