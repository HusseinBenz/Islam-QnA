How a person treats those who have wronged him, especially once he has power over them, shows his character more clearly than anything else. The life of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ gives many examples.

## Ta'if: the day he was stoned

After ten years of rejection in Mecca, he walked to the town of Ta'if to ask for a hearing. Its leaders mocked him and set the street children on him, who threw stones until his sandals filled with blood. He later called it the hardest day of his life.

The angel of the mountains then came to him and offered to crush the town between its two mountains. He replied: "No. I hope that God will bring from their descendants people who will worship God alone" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3231). Within a generation, the people of Ta'if were Muslims.

## Mecca: the day he had them in his hands

For thirteen years the leaders of Mecca had tortured his followers, starved his clan, killed people dear to him and driven him from his home. Eight years later he returned at the head of ten thousand men, and the city surrendered. He entered with his head bowed low in humility.

He then gathered the people who had persecuted him and asked what they expected of him. They said: "You are a noble brother, son of a noble brother." The early biographies record his reply, in the words Joseph spoke to his brothers: "No blame will there be upon you today" (Quran 12:92), and "Go, for you are free."

There were no reprisals and no forced conversions. Karen Armstrong is among the many biographers who point to this day as almost without parallel in the history of conquest.

## Everyday mercy

- **A prisoner.** Thumama ibn Uthal, a chief who had opposed him, was captured. The Prophet ﷺ treated him well for three days and then released him without conditions. Thumama went away, washed, and came back to embrace Islam of his own accord (Sahih al-Bukhari 4372).
- **A rough stranger.** A Bedouin pulled his cloak so hard that it marked his neck, and demanded money. He turned to him, smiled, and ordered that he be given something (Sahih al-Bukhari 3149).
- **A funeral.** When a Jewish funeral passed, he stood up. Told that the deceased was not a Muslim, he said: "Was it not a human soul?" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1312)
- **His household.** His wife Aisha said he never struck anyone with his hand, neither a woman nor a servant (Sahih Muslim 2328).

When some of his Companions asked him to curse his enemies, he answered: "I was not sent to curse. I was sent as a mercy" (Sahih Muslim 2599).

## Limits even in war

When fighting was forced upon him, he set rules that were centuries ahead of their time. He forbade the killing of women and children (Sahih al-Bukhari 3015), and his successor Abu Bakr, following his teaching, ordered his armies not to kill the elderly, not to cut down fruit trees, and to leave monks in their monasteries in peace.

## The principle behind it

> *"Good and evil are not equal. Repel evil with what is better, and the one between whom and you there was enmity will become like a devoted friend."* — Quran 41:34

That verse describes what actually happened. Khalid ibn al-Walid, who fought against him at Uhud, Abu Sufyan, who led the armies against him, and Ikrima, the son of his bitterest enemy, all ended their lives as his followers.

> *"We have not sent you except as a mercy to the worlds."* — Quran 21:107
