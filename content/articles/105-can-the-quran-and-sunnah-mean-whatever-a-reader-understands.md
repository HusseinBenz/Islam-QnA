Imagine two readers bring the same verse to settle a dispute and assign a different meaning to a key word. For the verse to help resolve anything, there must be a way to compare their readings.

Start with documented Arabic usage. Look at the surrounding passage, other uses of the term, and how the earliest readers understood it. Sometimes several readings can explain the evidence. In other cases the evidence narrows the possible meanings.

Al-Sayyid discusses Muhammad Shahrur's proposal to narrow the meaning of *zinā*, a term rendered as unlawful sexual intercourse in traditional law. Shahrur treats infidelity within marriage differently from private consensual sex between unmarried people. The dispute tests whether that restriction can explain how the term appears across the sources, including Quran 24:2. It also shows why the meaning of a word matters when readers draw legal or ethical conclusions from it.
