Muslims believe the Quran in their hands is, word for word, what the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ recited. This is a historical claim, and it can be checked.

## Preserved in two ways at once

From the first day, the Quran was protected by **memory** and by **writing** together.

- **Memory.** The Quran is recited aloud in the five daily prayers. Many Companions memorised all of it during the Prophet's lifetime, and the Prophet himself reviewed it with the angel Gabriel every year, and twice in his final year (Sahih al-Bukhari 4998).
- **Writing.** Whenever verses were revealed, the Prophet ﷺ called his scribes, among them Zayd ibn Thabit, and told them exactly where each passage belonged.

Each method checked the other. A scribe's slip would be caught by hundreds of memorisers. A lapse of memory would be caught by the written record.

## Compiled by eyewitnesses

Within about two years of the Prophet's death, the caliph Abu Bakr asked Zayd ibn Thabit to gather the written Quran into one collection. Zayd accepted a written passage only when it was supported by witnesses and agreed with what the memorisers knew (Sahih al-Bukhari 4986).

Around 650 CE, the caliph Uthman had a committee make official copies from that collection and sent them to the major cities (Sahih al-Bukhari 4987). All of this happened in public, among thousands of people who had learned the Quran from the Prophet's own mouth, and none of them objected that the text had been altered.

## What the manuscripts show

Early Quran manuscripts survive in Istanbul, Tashkent, Sana'a, Paris, London and elsewhere. In 2015 the University of Birmingham announced that leaves in its collection had been radiocarbon dated to between 568 and 645 CE, which is within or very close to the Prophet's lifetime. Their text matches the Quran read today.

Muhammad Mustafa al-A'zami lays out this evidence in detail in *The History of the Qur'anic Text*.

## A living chain

The strongest evidence is not in a museum. Today millions of people of every nationality, including children, know the entire Quran by heart. A reciter in Indonesia and a reciter in Morocco, who have never met and share no language, will recite the same words. Many hold a certificate (*ijazah*) naming every teacher in their chain back to the Prophet ﷺ. If every printed copy vanished tonight, the Quran could be written out again by morning.

## What about the different recitations?

There are a number of canonical recitations (*qira'at*), which differ in pronunciation and occasionally in a word. These are not later changes. The Prophet ﷺ himself taught the Quran in more than one mode to ease it for the different Arab tribes (Sahih al-Bukhari 4992), and each canonical recitation is traced back to him through its own chain.

## A promise kept

> *"Indeed, it is We who sent down the Reminder, and indeed, We will be its guardian."* — Quran 15:9

No other scripture of comparable age is preserved in its original language, memorised in full by millions, and identical across the world. The promise was made fourteen centuries ago, and anyone can verify that it has been kept.
