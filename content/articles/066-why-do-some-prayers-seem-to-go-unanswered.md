A person can ask sincerely and still wait, or never receive the particular thing they hoped for. The Qur’an’s invitation to pray sits alongside its teaching that human life includes hardship. A hadith addresses the discouragement of waiting and asks believers to avoid giving up because they have yet to see a result.

Al-Sayyid points to people who understand something that happened as an answer to prayer, and to times when a delay eventually made sense. He also explains why demanding a particular result as a test of God differs from praying in trust. Neither personal experience by itself settles whether God exists. The book asks readers to weigh that question against the broader case for belief.

Ibn al-Qayyim describes prayer’s effects as involving several conditions. The book draws from this the value of sincerity and self-reflection. A delay tells us very little about the reason for it or about the person who prayed. Someone can keep asking, reflect on what they can change, and seek practical help.
