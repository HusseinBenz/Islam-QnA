Debates about evolution and God often go badly because three different questions get mixed together. Separating them makes each one easier to think about.

## 1. The scientific question: what happened?

Biologists draw on fossils, comparative anatomy, genetics and observed changes in populations to support biological evolution and common ancestry. Natural selection is the process by which heritable traits that affect survival and reproduction become more or less common over generations. Selection is systematic, even though the genetic mutations it acts on arise without regard to what an organism needs. Scientists continue to debate many details, including how particular traits and structures evolved and how fast change occurs.

These are empirical questions, and they are answered by empirical evidence. Darwin himself proposed a test. If a complex organ existed that could not possibly have formed by numerous, successive, slight modifications, his theory would break down (*On the Origin of Species*, p. 189). Writers in the intelligent-design movement, such as Michael Behe and Jonathan Wells, have argued that some biological systems meet that test. Most biologists disagree, and the U.S. National Academies explain why they do not regard intelligent design as a scientific alternative. Pointing to complexity alone does not show that a system could not have arisen gradually, so this debate has to be settled by evidence about specific systems.

## 2. The philosophical question: does a process exclude a Creator?

This is a separate question, and it is where most of the real disagreement lies. Some writers present evolution as proof that no creator is needed: life's diversity "designed itself." The philosopher Antony Flew, for decades a leading atheist who later came to accept a Creator, discussed how evolution had been used in arguments for atheism. He also noted that the history of conflict between churches and scientists had coloured that debate.

The key point is the one made about [laws of nature](article.html?id=61). **Describing how a process works does not show that nothing stands behind it.** A process of variation and selection presupposes a great deal it does not explain: a universe with stable laws, chemistry capable of life, the first self-replicating systems, and genetic codes that carry information. The Qur'an speaks of God creating *"in stages"* (71:14) and of a creation that unfolds by His decree (54:49). It does not portray God as a craftsman competing with natural processes. Whether God works through gradual processes in biology is a question of what He did, not of whether He exists.

## 3. The theological question: what about Adam?

Here Muslims have their own question, which non-Muslims may not share. The Qur'an says:

> *"Indeed, the example of Jesus with God is like that of Adam. He created him from dust; then He said to him, 'Be,' and he was."* (Quran 3:59)

It describes God forming the first human from clay, proportioning him and breathing into him of His spirit (15:28–29; 38:71–72), and calls him *"the one I created with My hands"* (38:75).

The verse in 3:59 compares Adam with Jesus. Jesus had a mother and no father, and Adam had neither, which is the point of the comparison as al-Tabari and Ibn Kathir explain it. Most Muslim scholars therefore read these passages as teaching that Adam was **created directly**, without biological parents. Some modern Muslim writers accept evolution for other living things while holding to Adam's special creation. Others go further. These positions are readings of scripture, and they should be discussed as such. Biological common ancestry, by contrast, is evaluated by empirical evidence.

## Keeping the questions apart

- A person can accept a scientific finding and still believe God created the world.
- A person can question a scientific claim, but needs scientific evidence to do so.
- A person can hold a theological view about Adam on the basis of revelation, while being honest that this is a matter of revelation, not of biology.

Keeping these apart lets each conversation happen on its own terms. See also [claims that science and Islam conflict](article.html?id=122).
