**The scientific question.** Fossils, genetics and observed changes support biological evolution and common ancestry. Natural selection is the process by which heritable traits that affect reproduction become more or less common. Selection is systematic, even though mutations can arise without regard to what an organism needs. Biologists continue to debate details, including how particular traits evolved.

**The philosophical question.** The book cites Antony Flew’s account of how evolution is used in arguments for atheism and questions whether the history of conflict between churches and scientists can color that debate. It points to critiques by Michael Behe, Jonathan Wells and other intelligent-design writers. Behe takes up Darwin’s proposed test involving a complex organ that could not arise by successive changes. That claim needs evidence beyond a system's complexity. The National Academies explain why intelligent design has not met the evidentiary standard of a scientific alternative.

**The theological question.** Some Muslim writers understand evolution as one of the processes by which God creates. Al-Sayyid distinguishes that view from the particular claim that Adam had biological parents. He reads Qur’an 3:59, which compares Adam’s creation from dust with Jesus’s creation, as pointing to a direct creation of Adam. That is a reading of scripture. Biological common ancestry is evaluated by empirical evidence.
