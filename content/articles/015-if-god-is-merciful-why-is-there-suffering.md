This is the oldest and most human of all questions, and Islam does not treat it as an attack. The Quran records that even the angels asked it. When God announced the creation of humankind, they said: "Will You place on earth one who will cause corruption in it and shed blood?" God answered: "I know what you do not know" (2:30).

That reply is the beginning of the answer.

## We see a fragment of the picture

The objection assumes that if *we* cannot see a good reason for some suffering, there is none. The Quran challenges that assumption with the story of Moses and al-Khidr (18:60–82). Moses watches in horror as his companion damages the boat of poor fishermen. Later he learns that a tyrant was seizing every sound boat, and the damage saved their livelihood. What looked like harm was protection.

> *"Perhaps you dislike a thing and it is good for you, and perhaps you love a thing and it is bad for you. Allah knows, and you do not know."* — Quran 2:216

A child being vaccinated feels only the needle. The parent holding the child sees further. Our position before God is far smaller than the child's before its parent.

## This world was never meant to be Paradise

Islam teaches that life is a test, and a test without difficulty is not a test:

> *"We will surely test you with something of fear and hunger, and loss of wealth, lives and fruits. But give good news to the patient, who, when disaster strikes them, say: To Allah we belong, and to Him we shall return."* — Quran 2:155–156

Courage needs danger. Generosity needs someone in need. Patience, forgiveness and compassion, the qualities we admire most, can only grow in a world where things go wrong.

## Much evil is ours, and it will be answered

A great deal of suffering comes from human choices: war, greed, cruelty. God gave us real freedom, and real freedom can be abused. But the story does not end at the grave:

> *"Do not think that Allah is unaware of what the wrongdoers do. He only delays them until a Day when eyes will stare in horror."* — Quran 14:42

Without God and a final judgment, the murdered child and the murderer end in the same dust, and there is no justice anywhere. With God, every wrong is put right.

## No pain is wasted

The Prophet ﷺ said: "No fatigue, illness, worry, grief, harm or distress befalls a Muslim, not even the prick of a thorn, except that Allah removes some of his sins because of it" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5641). He also said that the people tested most severely are the prophets, then those nearest to them in goodness (Jami' al-Tirmidhi 2398). Hardship is not a sign that God has abandoned you.

And the scale is different from what we imagine. The Prophet ﷺ described the person who suffered most in this world being dipped once into Paradise and asked, "Did you ever see any hardship?" He will answer: "No, by Allah, my Lord" (Sahih Muslim 2807).

## To the one who is hurting now

An argument is not what a grieving heart needs first. So hear the promise as well:

> *"So truly, with hardship comes ease. Truly, with hardship comes ease."* — Quran 94:5–6

Ibn al-Qayyim wrote at length on the wisdom hidden in hardship in *Shifa' al-'Alil*. Jeffrey Lang, a mathematics professor who left atheism, took the title of his book *Even Angels Ask* from the verse above, because he found that the Quran had already voiced his hardest objection and answered it.
