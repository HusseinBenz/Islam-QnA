Many young people never voice their questions. They rarely meet a teacher they can approach, every subject is delivered as a lecture, or they expect to be told off for asking. The question does not go away. It simply moves to places where someone else will answer it.

## The Qur'an sets the tone

> *"Invite to the way of your Lord with wisdom and good instruction, and argue with them in the way that is best."* (Quran 16:125)

> *"It was by mercy from God that you were gentle with them. Had you been harsh and hard-hearted, they would have scattered from around you."* (Quran 3:159)

The second verse is addressed to the Prophet himself. If gentleness was required of him, it is required of every teacher.

## How the Prophet taught

**He asked questions before giving answers.** *"Do you know who the bankrupt one is?"* he asked his Companions, and then corrected their first answer (Sahih Muslim 2581). He asked them which tree resembles a believer and let them think before telling them it was the date palm (Sahih al-Bukhari 61).

**He used pictures.** He once drew a square in the sand, a line running through it and out beyond it, and small lines along its side. He explained that the square is a person's lifespan, the line beyond it is his hopes, and the small lines are the events that befall him (Sahih al-Bukhari 6417).

**He reasoned with a young man's temptation instead of rebuking it.** A young man asked his permission to commit fornication, and the people around him began to scold him. The Prophet ﷺ brought him close and asked, *"Would you like it for your mother? Your daughter? Your sister?"* Each time the young man said no, and the Prophet replied that other people do not like it for theirs either. Then he placed his hand on him and prayed for him (Musnad Ahmad 22211).

**He did not shame newcomers.** Mu'awiya ibn al-Hakam spoke during prayer without knowing it was forbidden, and the congregation glared at him. Afterwards the Prophet explained the rule calmly. Mu'awiya said: *"I have never seen a teacher before or after him who taught better. By God, he did not scold me, strike me or revile me"* (Sahih Muslim 537).

**He spoke to people at their level.** 'Ali ibn Abi Talib advised: *"Speak to people according to what they understand. Do you want God and His Messenger to be denied?"* (Sahih al-Bukhari 127).

## Five marks of good religious teaching

1. **Explain the reasoning**, not only the conclusion. People hold more firmly to what they understand.
2. **Find out what is actually being asked.** Restate the question and ask, "Have I understood you?" The reply often changes once the question is clear.
3. **Match examples and depth to the listener.** A twelve-year-old and a philosophy student need different answers to the same question.
4. **Be fair, even in disagreement.** Represent the other view accurately and give evidence, not just authority.
5. **Care about the person.** The aim is their good, not winning the exchange.

## Practical formats

Open question sessions, where the audience sets the agenda, have proven effective. Public preachers such as Ahmed Deedat and Zakir Naik became known for them. Small study circles, anonymous question boxes and one-to-one conversations each reach people a lecture never will. Teachers also need to know when to say "I don't know, let me find out." The [early scholars](article.html?id=50) considered that a mark of knowledge, not of weakness.
