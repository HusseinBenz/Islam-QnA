How can someone who never met Muhammad follow his teaching? Al-Shafiʿi begins with that practical question. The Quran tells believers to turn to God and the Messenger in a dispute (4:59). Sunni jurists understand this as turning to the Quran and to Muhammad's guidance, which later generations can reach through reliable reports.

Al-Sayyid also cites traditions in which Muhammad warns against dismissing a directive simply because it appears outside the Quran. One report comes through Abu Rafiʿ, another through al-Miqdam. These reports carry weight for readers who accept the relevant transmission. For readers questioning hadith altogether, the Quranic argument and the historical case for transmission need to be considered separately.

Finally, the book points to early scholarly practice. Al-Kinani explains how Quran 4:59 was understood after Muhammad's death. Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr reports broad agreement among the jurists and hadith scholars he knew on using a reliable report with a limited chain of transmission when nothing supersedes it. Sunni scholars still examine each report's authenticity, legal meaning and relation to other evidence.
