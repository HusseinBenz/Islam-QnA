How can someone who never met Muhammad follow his teaching? That practical question is where al-Shafi'i, the founder of one of the four Sunni schools, began his debate with people who accepted the Qur'an but rejected the reports of the Sunnah. He recorded the debate in *Jima' al-'Ilm*. The Sunni case can be set out in three layers.

## 1. The Qur'an's own commands

The Qur'an does not present the Prophet as a mere courier who delivers a book and steps aside. It gives him an ongoing role:

- **Explainer:** *"We sent down to you the Reminder so that you may make clear to the people what was sent down to them"* (16:44).
- **Authority to obey:** *"Obey God and obey the Messenger"* (4:59), and *"Whoever obeys the Messenger has obeyed God"* (4:80).
- **Judge in disputes:** *"If you disagree over anything, refer it to God and the Messenger"* (4:59), and *"they will not believe until they make you judge"* (4:65).
- **Model:** *"There has certainly been for you in the Messenger of God an excellent example"* (33:21).
- **Source of binding instruction:** *"Whatever the Messenger gives you, take it"* (59:7). *"Let those who oppose his command beware"* (24:63).

Sunni jurists understand "refer it to God and the Messenger" after his death as referring to the Qur'an and to the Prophet's guidance. Later generations reach that guidance through reliable reports. Ibn Hazm (*al-Ihkam*) and Ibn al-Qayyim (*I'lam al-Muwaqqi'in*) develop this argument at length. The command to obey the Messenger was not limited to those who could see him.

## 2. The Prophet's warnings

Several reports record the Prophet ﷺ anticipating the objection:

> *"Let me not find one of you reclining on his couch, when a command of mine reaches him, something I have ordered or forbidden, and he says: 'I do not know. Whatever we find in the Book of God, we follow.'"* (narrated by Abu Rafi': Sunan Abi Dawud 4605; Jami' al-Tirmidhi 2663; Sunan Ibn Majah 13)

> *"Indeed, I have been given the Book and something like it along with it."* (narrated by al-Miqdam ibn Ma'dikarib: Sunan Abi Dawud 4604; Musnad Ahmad)

These reports carry weight for readers who accept the relevant chains of transmission. For readers who doubt hadith altogether, they cannot be the first step, because that would assume what is being questioned. That is why the **Qur'anic argument** (layer 1) and the **historical case for transmission** ([see here](article.html?id=100)) need to be considered on their own terms.

## 3. The practice of the earliest generations

The third layer is historical. The Companions and their students acted on reports of the Prophet's teaching from the beginning. They asked one another what he had said or done, and they changed their practice when a reliable report reached them.

- 'Abd al-'Aziz al-Kinani's *al-Hayda* records a debate at the court of the caliph al-Ma'mun. In it he explains how Qur'an 4:59 was understood after the Prophet's death, as referral to the Qur'an and to the Sunnah.
- Ibn 'Abd al-Barr, in the introduction to *al-Tamhid*, reports agreement among the jurists and hadith scholars he knew on acting on a reliable report transmitted by individuals (*khabar al-wahid*), when nothing supersedes it.
- Al-Shafi'i, in *Jima' al-'Ilm*, pressed his opponent to explain how he could carry out the Qur'an's own commands, such as the prayer and the alms, without the details transmitted from the Prophet.

## What accepting the Sunnah does not mean

Accepting the Sunnah as a source does not mean accepting every report attributed to the Prophet. Sunni scholars still examine:

- **Authenticity:** is this report sound? ([See here](article.html?id=101).)
- **Legal meaning:** what does it establish, and is it general or specific?
- **Relation to other evidence:** does it qualify, explain or appear to conflict with another text? ([See here](article.html?id=102).)

Mustafa al-Siba'i's *al-Sunna wa-Makanatuha fi al-Tashri' al-Islami* is a good modern survey of the argument and of the objections raised against it. See also [if the Qur'an explains everything, why the Sunnah?](article.html?id=94)
