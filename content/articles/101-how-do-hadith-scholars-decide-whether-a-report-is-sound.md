Two trustworthy people can remember the same event differently. Hadith criticism asks how a difference arose and what other evidence could settle it. The book outlines five standard conditions for a *ṣaḥīḥ* or sound hadith.

1. **Integrity.** Were the transmitters considered honest and reliable?
2. **Accuracy.** Did they usually retain and convey what they received?
3. **Continuity.** Does the evidence support each link in the chain?
4. **Consistency with stronger versions.** Does the report diverge from more reliable accounts in a significant way?
5. **Freedom from hidden defects.** Does close comparison disclose a subtle error in the chain or wording?

Reliability can vary even within one person's work. A narrator might be accurate with one teacher's reports and less accurate with another's, or remember things less well later in life. Critics compare versions, weigh better-attested readings, and ask whether an otherwise honest but inconsistent narrator is supported by independent transmission. Scholars may grade a report differently if they weigh those clues differently.
