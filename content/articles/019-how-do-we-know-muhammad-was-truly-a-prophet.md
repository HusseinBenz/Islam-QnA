A claim to prophecy is the largest claim a human being can make. It deserves to be examined with the same care we would give any serious claim: by looking at the man, his motives, his message and his results.

## The man: known as "the Trustworthy"

For forty years before his mission, the people of Mecca called Muhammad ﷺ *al-Amin*, the Trustworthy, and left their valuables in his keeping. When he first called them publicly, he asked: "If I told you that horsemen were about to attack from behind this valley, would you believe me?" They answered: "We have never known you to lie" (Sahih al-Bukhari 4770).

The Byzantine emperor Heraclius reasoned the same way. He questioned Abu Sufyan, who was then the Prophet's enemy, and learned that Muhammad had never been accused of lying, had never broken a treaty, and that his followers were growing and none left out of dislike for the religion. Heraclius concluded that a man who does not lie about people would not lie about God (Sahih al-Bukhari 7).

## The motive: he gained nothing

The leaders of Mecca offered him wealth, kingship and marriage into any family if he would stop preaching. He refused, and endured thirteen years of mockery, boycott and violence instead.

When he later ruled all of Arabia, his life did not change. His wife Aisha said that months would pass without a cooking fire being lit in his household, and they lived on dates and water (Sahih al-Bukhari 2567). He slept on a rough mat that left marks on his side (Sahih al-Bukhari 4913). When he died, his armour was in pawn for some barley (Sahih al-Bukhari 2916).

Impostors seek money, power or pleasure. He had all three within reach, and declined them.

## The message and the results

He brought the Quran, which he could not have written (see "Did Muhammad ﷺ write the Quran himself?"). And in twenty-three years he turned warring, idol-worshipping tribes that buried infant daughters alive into a single community built on the worship of one God, literacy, charity and the rule of law. That community went on to shape world history.

## What historians outside Islam concluded

The Scottish writer Thomas Carlyle told his Victorian audience that the old charge of imposture could no longer be believed:

> *"A false man found a religion? Why, a false man cannot build a brick house!"* — Thomas Carlyle, *On Heroes* (1841)

The French poet and statesman Alphonse de Lamartine wrote:

> *"If greatness of purpose, smallness of means, and astounding results are the three criteria of human genius, who could dare to compare any great man in modern history with Muhammad?"* — *Histoire de la Turquie* (1854)

Michael H. Hart, ranking the most influential people in history, placed him first, calling him the only man in history "supremely successful on both the religious and secular levels". And W. Montgomery Watt wrote that to suppose him an impostor "raises more problems than it solves".

## Three options

Either he lied, or he was mistaken about his own experience, or he was telling the truth. Liars do not live and die in poverty for their lie. The deluded do not produce a coherent scripture, a legal system and a lasting civilisation. What remains is what he said of himself: a human being, and the Messenger of God.

To meet him properly, read a full biography. Martin Lings' *Muhammad* and al-Mubarakpuri's *The Sealed Nectar* are two of the best known.
