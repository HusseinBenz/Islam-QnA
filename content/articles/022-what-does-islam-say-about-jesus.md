Many people are surprised to learn that no one can be a Muslim without believing in Jesus, peace be upon him. He is named in the Quran some twenty-five times. His mother Mary is the only woman the Quran mentions by name, and an entire chapter, the nineteenth, is called after her.

## What Muslims believe about him

- **He was born of a virgin.** The angels told Mary that God had chosen her above the women of all the worlds (3:42) and gave her news of a son, though no man had touched her (3:45–47).
- **He is the Messiah**, and the Quran calls him a word from God and a spirit from Him (4:171).
- **He performed miracles by God's permission**: healing the blind and the leper, raising the dead, and speaking as an infant in the cradle to defend his mother (3:49, 19:29–33).
- **He will return** before the end of time, as the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ foretold (Sahih al-Bukhari 2222).

## Where Islam differs

Islam teaches that Jesus was a human messenger and not God or the son of God:

> *"The Messiah, son of Mary, was no more than a messenger; other messengers had passed away before him. And his mother was a woman of truth. They both used to eat food."* — Quran 5:75

His miraculous birth does not make him divine. Adam had neither father nor mother:

> *"The example of Jesus before Allah is like that of Adam. He created him from dust, then said to him, 'Be', and he was."* — Quran 3:59

The Quran also states that Jesus was not killed or crucified, though it was made to appear so, and that God raised him to Himself (4:157–158).

## What Jesus himself said

Muslims find this picture supported by words attributed to Jesus in the Gospels:

- Asked for the greatest commandment, he began: "Hear, O Israel: the Lord our God, the Lord is one" (Mark 12:29).
- "Why do you call me good? No one is good except God alone" (Mark 10:18).
- "This is eternal life: that they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom you have sent" (John 17:3).
- "I am ascending to my Father and your Father, to my God and your God" (John 20:17).
- In the garden he fell with his face to the ground and prayed (Matthew 26:39), which is how Muslims pray to this day.

The doctrine of the Trinity was defined in church councils centuries later. The one verse that states it most plainly (1 John 5:7 in older Bibles) is absent from the earliest Greek manuscripts, a point explained by the New Testament scholar Bart Ehrman in *Misquoting Jesus*.

## A meeting point

When the first Muslims fled persecution to Christian Abyssinia, the king asked what they believed about Jesus. Ja'far ibn Abi Talib recited to him from the chapter of Mary. The early biographies record that the king wept until his beard was wet, and said that this and what Jesus brought came from the same source.

Islam does not ask a Christian to give up Jesus. It asks them to follow him in what he taught: to worship the God he worshipped.

> *"He said: I am the servant of Allah. He has given me the Scripture and made me a prophet."* — Quran 19:30
