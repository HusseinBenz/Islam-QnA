A thermometer can measure temperature. Logic can tell you whether a conclusion follows from its premises. Testimony can establish events you did not witness, if it can be checked and trusted. These tools often work together. You can reason about what an observation shows and use independent reports to confirm it.

Al-Sayyid argues that some debates about God turn on what participants accept as evidence. If one person allows only experimental knowledge, rational arguments and testimony will not count for them. Ask whether that rule itself can be established by experiment, and what historical or everyday knowledge the rule would rule out.

In the book’s Sunni framework, the Quran and genuinely established Sunnah are God’s guidance. Authenticity and interpretation need work, and a verified consensus has a different standing from a finding by an individual jurist. Even an eminent scholar can err. The author suggests studying why a source deserves trust before following its guidance.
