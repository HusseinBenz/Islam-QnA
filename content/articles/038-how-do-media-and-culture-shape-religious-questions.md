Al-Sayyid identifies five influences. Social media can circulate information quickly and reward controversy. Films and novels can make assumptions feel familiar by embedding them in stories. Studying or working across cultures brings people into contact with new values and teachers, which gives them ideas to examine as well as opportunities to learn. Attractive habits can also bring a person’s choices and convictions into tension. Islamic scripture presents repeated wrongdoing and repentance as matters that can shape the heart.

The fifth influence is cultural prestige. Sigrid Hunke recounts a Christian writer in medieval Córdoba lamenting the prestige of Arabic literature among young Christians. Ibn Khaldun described how communities imitate powerful societies. Abdullah al-Ujayri applies the idea to modern readings of Islam shaped by dominant socialist, military, democratic, or liberal ideals. Al-Sayyid’s practical question is whether a value can be defended on its own terms once we set aside the prestige of the society associated with it.
