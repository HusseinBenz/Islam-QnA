Look across a large field and the ground seems flat for as far as you can see. Seen at the scale of the whole planet, that same ground is a tiny part of a curved surface. Both descriptions are true, each at its own scale.

## What the verses say

Several verses describe the earth in terms of human experience:

> *"And it is He who spread out the earth (madda al-ard) and placed in it firm mountains and rivers."* (Quran 13:3)

> *"And the earth, We spread it out and cast in it firm mountains."* (Quran 15:19)

> *"And at the earth, how it was laid out (sutihat)."* (Quran 88:20)

These verses address people living on the earth and ask them to notice how it has been made habitable. It is broad, walkable and able to be cultivated. That is a description of the earth *as it is for us*. It is not a claim about its overall geometric shape.

## Al-Razi: a sphere looks flat up close

The great theologian and commentator Fakhr al-Din al-Razi addressed exactly this question in *Mafatih al-Ghayb*, in his commentary on 13:3. He wrote that the earth's being "spread out" does not contradict its being a sphere. A very large sphere, seen by someone standing on a small part of it, appears as a flat surface. Since the earth is immense, the part each person sees looks level, and that is the sense of "spread out."

He wrote this in the twelfth century, not in response to modern science.

## Ibn Hazm and Ibn Taymiyya on the earth's shape

The Andalusian scholar Ibn Hazm, in *al-Fasl fi al-Milal*, reported that none of the Muslim scholars he regarded as leading authorities denied that the earth is spherical. He cited evidence from the Qur'an, such as the verse saying God *"wraps the night over the day and wraps the day over the night"* (39:5). The verb *yukawwiru* is related to *kura*, "ball," and describes wrapping something around a rounded object. Ibn Taymiyya likewise reported, in his fatwas, the agreement of earlier scholars that the heavens and the earth are round.

So the claim that classical Muslims read these verses as teaching a flat earth is mistaken as a matter of history. Leading scholars explicitly considered the question and concluded the opposite.

## What *daḥāhā* means

Qur'an 79:30 says: *"And after that He spread it out (daḥāhā)."* Some modern writers have suggested that *daḥā* refers to an egg shape. That reading is not well supported by classical Arabic dictionaries or commentaries, which explain the word as spreading out, smoothing and making habitable, and the next verses continue in that sense: *"He brought forth from it its water and its pasture, and the mountains He set firmly"* (79:31–32). The verse does not need a strained reading to be consistent with a round earth. Its plain meaning, the earth made broad and habitable, already is.

## The general principle

As with [the sun setting in a spring](article.html?id=71), the Qur'an speaks to people in the language of their experience. Reading that language as a technical statement of geometry misunderstands it. Classical scholars understood this, and some of them stated it in so many words.
