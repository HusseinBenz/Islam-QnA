The equations of aerodynamics explain how a plane flies. Knowing those equations still leaves questions about how that plane came to be. Al-Sayyid uses the comparison to separate two jobs an explanation can do.

In science, a law can describe a stable pattern and help explain or predict what happens under particular conditions. A model of gravity, for example, helps predict an orbit. The philosophical question is why there is a universe with those regularities in the first place. The book argues that describing the regularities by itself cannot establish that the universe created itself.

The analogy helps clarify the question. The case for a creator depends on independent arguments about why an ultimate explanation is needed and what sort of explanation could answer it.
