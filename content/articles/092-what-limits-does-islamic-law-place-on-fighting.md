When an armed group calls itself Islamic, its label tells you how it describes itself. It does not tell you whether its actions follow Islamic teaching. To assess that, compare what the group does with what the sources say. The same standard applies to every worldview. Crimes committed by regimes that espoused atheism, for example, belong to the people who committed them, not to everyone who shares the worldview.

## The basic principle: no aggression

> *"Fight in the way of God those who fight you, but do not transgress. Indeed, God does not love transgressors."* (Quran 2:190)

> *"God does not forbid you from those who do not fight you because of religion and do not expel you from your homes, from being righteous toward them and acting justly toward them."* (Quran 60:8)

Classical jurists debated the exact grounds of legitimate war, but they agreed that fighting is bound by law. It is not a licence for unlimited violence. For the wider question, see [what jihad really means](article.html?id=26).

## Who may not be targeted

- **Women and children.** Ibn 'Umar reports that a woman was found killed in one of the Prophet's campaigns, and the Prophet ﷺ **forbade the killing of women and children** (Sahih al-Bukhari 3015; Sahih Muslim 1744).
- **Non-combatants generally.** Ibn Qudama records in *al-Mughni* that the jurists extended this protection to the elderly, monks in their retreats, the blind and the chronically ill, and peasants who take no part in fighting.
- **People under treaty.** *"Whoever kills a person under a covenant (mu'ahad) will not smell the fragrance of Paradise"* (Sahih al-Bukhari 3166).

## How war may be conducted

When the Prophet ﷺ appointed a commander, he instructed him: *"Fight in the name of God... Do not steal from the spoils, do not act treacherously, do not mutilate, and do not kill a child"* (Sahih Muslim 1731). Other reports confirm the prohibition of plunder and mutilation (Sahih al-Bukhari 2474).

## Captives and those seeking protection

The Qur'an praises those who *"give food, despite loving it, to the poor, the orphan and the captive"* (76:8).

It also commands protection for an enemy who asks for it:

> *"And if any of the polytheists seeks your protection, grant him protection so that he may hear the word of God, then escort him to his place of safety."* (Quran 9:6)

Ibn Kathir explains that such a person is to be kept safe while he hears the message, and then returned safely to his own territory whether he accepts it or not.

## The case of Usama

During a battle, Usama ibn Zayd overpowered an enemy fighter who then declared *"There is no god but God."* Usama killed him anyway, thinking he had said it only out of fear. When the Prophet ﷺ heard, he asked again and again: *"Did you kill him after he said 'There is no god but God'?"* Usama said, *"He only said it to protect himself."* The Prophet replied: *"Did you split open his heart to know whether he meant it?"* (Sahih Muslim 96; Sahih al-Bukhari 6872). The report sets a high bar against killing on assumption.

## The aims and the institutions

Classical scholars described the purposes of historical warfare as defending the community, protecting the freedom to convey the message, and removing barriers that prevented people from choosing their religion. They held at the same time that *"there is no compulsion in religion"* (2:256). They also discussed *jizya*, a tax paid by certain non-Muslim communities under arrangements of protection, under which those communities kept their religion and their own laws (9:29). These are claims about specific texts and institutions. Accounts of particular wars, in any period, still need evidence about what the actors actually did and why.

## The test

When someone claims that Islam endorses indiscriminate violence, or claims to act in Islam's name, ask:

1. Does the action target women, children, non-combatants or people under treaty?
2. Does it involve treachery, mutilation or killing captives?
3. Does it ignore the protection owed to those who seek safety?

By the standard of the sources, any one of these is a violation, whoever commits it. See also [does Islam promote violence?](article.html?id=3)
