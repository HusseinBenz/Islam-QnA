If you are asking this question because of something you have done, read this verse slowly. Many scholars have called it the most hopeful verse in the Quran:

> *"Say: O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of the mercy of Allah. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins. Indeed, it is He who is the Forgiving, the Merciful."* — Quran 39:53

God calls them *My servants* while they are still in their sin. He does not say "some sins". He says *all*.

## How wide is His mercy?

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ conveyed these words from God, the last hadith in al-Nawawi's *Forty*:

> *"O son of Adam, as long as you call upon Me and hope in Me, I will forgive you whatever you have done, and I will not mind. O son of Adam, if your sins were to reach the clouds of the sky and you then asked My forgiveness, I would forgive you."* — Jami' al-Tirmidhi 3540

He once saw a mother who had lost her child in the confusion after a battle. She found the child, clasped it to her chest and nursed it. He asked his Companions: "Do you think this woman would throw her child into the fire?" They said, "No." He said: "God is more merciful to His servants than this mother is to her child" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5999).

He also said that God divided mercy into a hundred parts and sent down just one, by which all creatures show compassion to each other. He has kept ninety-nine for the Day of Resurrection (Sahih Muslim 2752).

## The man who killed a hundred

The Prophet ﷺ told of a man who had murdered ninety-nine people and asked whether he could still repent. The first person he asked said no, and he killed him too. Then he asked a scholar, who said: "What could stand between you and repentance?" The man set out for a town of good people to begin again, and died on the road. God forgave him, because he had turned toward Him (Sahih Muslim 2766).

## Nobody in between

In Islam there is no confession to a priest and no one whose permission you need. You speak to God directly, wherever you are, in any language:

> *"When My servants ask you about Me: I am near. I answer the call of the caller when he calls on Me."* — Quran 2:186

## What repentance requires

Al-Nawawi summarises the conditions in *Riyad al-Salihin*:

1. Stop the sin.
2. Feel real regret for it.
3. Resolve not to go back to it.
4. If you wronged another person, put it right: return what was taken, or ask their pardon.

If you fall again, repent again. God does not tire of forgiving until you tire of asking.

## Does that include the worst sins?

The Quran says God does not forgive associating partners with Him for a person who dies upon it without repenting (4:48). While a person lives, even that is wiped away by turning back. Most of the Prophet's Companions had been idol-worshippers.

The reward goes beyond pardon:

> *"Except for those who repent, believe and do righteous deeds. For them Allah will replace their evil deeds with good."* — Quran 25:70

## Do not wait

The door stays open as long as you are alive. The Prophet ﷺ said that God stretches out His hand at night to accept the repentance of those who sinned by day, and by day for those who sinned at night (Sahih Muslim 2759).

Despair, the feeling of being "too far gone", is itself a mistake about who God is. Ibn al-Qayyim devotes some of the most beautiful pages of *Madarij al-Salikin* to this.
