1. **Observe the natural world.** Consider the sky, the human body, and animal life. A well-made documentary or a short research project can help you attend to details you usually pass over.
2. **Read the Quran for its argument.** Trace how a passage connects the purpose of life, resurrection, or God’s qualities to the reasons it gives. Use a reliable commentary when you need context.
3. **Read about the foundations.** Try a work on the case for God, the Quran’s origin, or Muhammad’s prophethood. A reading group can compare and summarize the arguments.
4. **Learn how Islam describes God.** Alongside why Muslims worship God alone, ask what the Quran says about God as creator, sustainer, and judge. The first verses of Sūrat al-‘Alaq begin with God as creator.
5. **Practise inward worship.** For Muslims this includes trust, humility, gratitude, love, reverence, and hope. The book sees Abraham’s settled conviction in Quran 6:80–82 as an example.
6. **Listen to people who came to Islam later.** Their stories can show why a passage or practice mattered to them and bring familiar teachings into focus. Personal testimony illustrates an experience.
