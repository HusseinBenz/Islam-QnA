Look at *al-ṣābiʾūn* in Qur’an 5:69 and *al-ṣābiʾīn* in 2:62 and 22:17. English usually gives all three as “the Sabians,” but in Arabic the last syllable helps show what work a word is doing in the sentence.

Ibn Ashur records the common analysis that treats *al-ṣābiʾūn* as the independent subject of an implied statement. To convey how this works, imagine saying “those groups are subject to this condition, and the Sabians too,” but mentioning the Sabians earlier in the sentence. An early Arabic verse by Ḍābiʾ ibn al-Ḥārith is used to illustrate the same construction. The other two verses use an ordinary coordinated list. The comparison shows why the endings can differ even though the noun refers to the same group.
