Can I ask a difficult question without already having the answer? *Sabighat* says the space for sincere inquiry is wide. The Quran portrays Abraham asking to see how God raises the dead so he can feel reassured (2:260). Elsewhere it uses the example of asking people familiar with earlier scripture (10:94). Al-Sayyid asks teachers to keep inquiry and dialogue open.

The author treats public speech as a further question. He points to the historical coexistence of Muslims and non-Muslims under Islamic rule and argues that coexistence could include protected religious practice alongside restrictions on public advocacy or attacks on Islam. He also treats honest inquiry and public efforts to spread religious doubts differently. These are claims about how he interprets religious duties and historical legal arrangements. A record of communities living together establishes their presence and interaction. The exact rights they had call for evidence about the place, period, and legal arrangement.
