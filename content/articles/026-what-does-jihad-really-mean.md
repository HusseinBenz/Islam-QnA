Few words are as misunderstood as *jihad*. It is often translated as "holy war", a phrase that does not occur anywhere in the Quran.

## The word

*Jihad* comes from a root meaning to strive or to exert effort. The Quran uses it in Mecca, years before any fighting was allowed, telling the Prophet ﷺ to strive against falsehood "with it", meaning with the Quran, "a great striving" (25:52). That is a jihad of words and argument.

The Prophet ﷺ described its forms:

- "The one who strives (*mujahid*) is the one who strives against his own self in obedience to God" (Jami' al-Tirmidhi 1621).
- "The best jihad is a word of truth in front of a tyrannical ruler" (Sunan Abi Dawud 4344).
- A man asked permission to go out to fight. The Prophet ﷺ asked, "Are your parents alive?" He said yes. "Then strive in serving them" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3004).

Ibn al-Qayyim, in *Zad al-Ma'ad*, lists the levels of jihad in order: against one's own soul, against the whisperings of Satan, then against wrongdoing by speaking and acting for what is right. Armed struggle is one part of that list.

## When fighting is allowed

For thirteen years in Mecca, Muslims were tortured and killed and were not allowed to fight back. Permission came only after they had been driven from their homes:

> *"Permission is given to those who are fought against, because they have been wronged."* — Quran 22:39

The governing rule followed:

> *"Fight in the way of Allah those who fight you, but do not transgress. Indeed, Allah does not love transgressors."* — Quran 2:190

> *"If they incline to peace, then incline to it also."* — Quran 8:61

## The "sword verse" in its context

Quran 9:5, "kill the polytheists wherever you find them", is the verse most often quoted to accuse Islam. Read the passage around it:

- **9:1–4** address particular Arab tribes who had signed treaties with the Muslims and broken them. Verse 4 explicitly exempts those who kept their treaties.
- **9:5** applies to the treaty-breakers, after a four-month warning.
- **9:6**, the very next verse: "If any one of the polytheists seeks your protection, grant it to him so that he may hear the word of Allah, then escort him to his place of safety."
- **9:13** names the reason: they "broke their oaths", plotted to expel the Messenger, and "attacked you first".

It is a ruling about a specific enemy in a specific war. It is not a command about non-Muslims in general, which is governed by 60:8.

## Rules of war

When Abu Bakr, the first caliph, sent out an army, he instructed it, as recorded in Malik's *Muwatta'*: do not kill a woman, a child or an old man; do not cut down fruit-bearing trees; do not destroy inhabited places; do not kill livestock except for food; do not burn palm trees; and leave in peace those who have devoted themselves to worship in monasteries.

## Terrorism is the opposite of jihad

Deliberately killing civilians violates every rule above. The Quran equates the killing of one innocent person with the killing of all humanity (5:32).

Ordinary Muslims know this. John Esposito and Dalia Mogahed, reporting Gallup's poll of Muslims in more than thirty-five countries in *Who Speaks for Islam?*, found that the great majority condemned attacks on civilians, and that many of them gave religious reasons for doing so.
