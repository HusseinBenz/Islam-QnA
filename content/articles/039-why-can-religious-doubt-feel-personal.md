Al-Sayyid describes six possible influences. **Unexamined conviction** can be fragile, and he suggests that neglecting prayer can sometimes reflect this. **Hardship** can make questions about God’s justice urgent. **A lack of spiritual practice** can leave someone with little experience of trust, love, humility, or worship. **Limited research habits** make claims harder to check. **Gaps in religious knowledge** make it difficult to place a claim in context. **Unstructured time** can leave room for recurring worries that meaningful activity may help interrupt.

The author draws on conversations and informal responses from his courses. They offer possible explanations, and each reader’s circumstances deserve attention. Quran 22:11 describes a person whose trust depends on things going well. Ibn Abbas illustrates it with people judging faith by their family’s or livestock’s fortunes after arriving in Medina. Al-Sa‘di connects the example to the challenge of trusting through hardship. A reader in distress may need a patient listener before they can benefit from a technical answer.
