The Quran makes a claim that can be explored by anyone with a Bible: that the coming of the final Prophet was announced in the earlier scriptures.

> *"Those who follow the Messenger, the unlettered Prophet, whom they find written in what they have of the Torah and the Gospel."* — Quran 7:157

Muslim scholars have studied this question for centuries, most famously Rahmatullah Kairanawi in *Izhar al-Haqq*. These are the passages they point to most often. The biblical wording below follows common English translations.

## 1. A prophet like Moses

God says to Moses:

> *"I will raise up for them a prophet like you from among their brothers. I will put my words in his mouth, and he will tell them everything I command him."* — Deuteronomy 18:18

Muslims note three things. **"Like you"**: Muhammad ﷺ, like Moses, was born naturally, married, led his people out of persecution, brought a complete law, governed a community, and died a natural death. **"From among their brothers"**: the brothers of the Israelites, descendants of Isaac, are the descendants of his brother Ishmael, the ancestor of the Arabs. **"My words in his mouth"**: this is an exact description of an unlettered man reciting revelation word for word.

## 2. The servant in the land of Kedar

Isaiah 42 describes a chosen servant who brings justice to the nations, whose law the distant lands await, and at whose coming the desert rejoices:

> *"Let the wilderness and its towns raise their voices; let the settlements where Kedar lives rejoice."* — Isaiah 42:11

Kedar was a son of Ishmael (Genesis 25:13), and his descendants lived in Arabia.

## 3. "Are you the Prophet?"

When John the Baptist appeared, the religious leaders asked him three separate questions: Are you the Messiah? Are you Elijah? "Are you the Prophet?" (John 1:19–21). They were expecting three different figures. Jesus was the Messiah. Who, then, was "the Prophet"?

## 4. The one who comes after Jesus

Jesus tells his disciples:

> *"I have much more to say to you, more than you can now bear. But when he, the Spirit of truth, comes, he will guide you into all the truth. He will not speak on his own; he will speak only what he hears."* — John 16:12–13

The Quran reports that Jesus gave good news of a messenger to come after him (61:6). Muslims observe that "he will speak only what he hears" matches the way the Quran was delivered.

## An honest note

Christians generally understand the passage in John as referring to the Holy Spirit, and Jews and Christians read Deuteronomy and Isaiah in their own ways. Muslims do not rest their faith on these readings. The evidence for Muhammad's prophethood stands on his life and on the Quran itself.

But the passages are real, and the question they raise is a fair one. If God promised a final prophet from among the brothers of Israel, with God's words in his mouth, bringing a law for the nations, then it is worth asking seriously who has fitted that description.

> *"Say: O People of the Scripture, come to a word that is equitable between us and you: that we will worship none but Allah."* — Quran 3:64
