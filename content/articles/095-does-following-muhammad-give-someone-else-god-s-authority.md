Some people argue that following the Sunnah is a form of associating others with God. Only God judges, they say, so taking rulings from a human being, even a prophet, gives a creature God's authority. The argument usually starts from one verse.

## The verse

> *"Shall I seek a judge other than God, when it is He who has sent down to you the Book explained in detail?"* (Quran 6:114)

Ibn Kathir and Ibn 'Ashur explain the context. It is a dispute between the Prophet and the Meccan polytheists, who wanted him to accept some arbiter other than the revelation. The Prophet answers that God's revelation is the judge between them. The verse rejects any authority set up **against** God's revelation. It does not reject every human role in conveying or applying it.

## The same Qur'an assigns human judgment

The Qur'an uses the same language of judging for human beings acting within God's law:

- **Family disputes:** *"If you fear a breach between the two, appoint an arbiter (hakam) from his family and an arbiter from hers"* (4:35).
- **Compensation for hunting in the sacred state:** it is to be judged by *"two just men among you"* (5:95).
- **Judges generally:** *"When you judge between people, judge with justice"* (4:58).

If any human role in judgment were a rival to God, these verses would contradict 6:114. They do not, because these tasks **sit within** God's commands. The authority comes from God's instruction.

## The Messenger's authority comes from God

The same distinction explains prophetic authority in Sunni thought. The Qur'an itself commands:

> *"O you who believe, obey God and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you. And if you disagree over anything, refer it to God and the Messenger."* (Quran 4:59)

> *"But no, by your Lord, they will not believe until they make you judge concerning what arises between them, and then find no discomfort in themselves about what you have decided, and submit in full submission."* (Quran 4:65)

> *"Whoever obeys the Messenger has obeyed God."* (Quran 4:80)

The Messenger's authority is **not independent** of God's. He has it because God commissioned him to deliver and explain the message: *"Nor does he speak from desire. It is only a revelation revealed"* (53:3–4). Obeying him is obedience to the One who sent him. Refusing to follow him in the name of God's sole authority actually rejects God's own instruction.

## What the Qur'an does condemn

There is a real danger the Qur'an warns against:

> *"They have taken their scholars and monks as lords besides God."* (Quran 9:31)

When 'Adi ibn Hatim, a former Christian, heard this, he said, "We did not worship them." The Prophet ﷺ asked: *"Did they not make unlawful what God made lawful, and you made it unlawful, and make lawful what God made unlawful, and you made it lawful?"* He said yes. The Prophet said: *"That is your worship of them"* (Jami' al-Tirmidhi 3095).

That is the line. Giving any human being authority to **override** God's law is what the Qur'an condemns. Following a messenger whom God Himself commanded people to follow, in what he conveyed from God, is obedience to God. Scholars come after him. Their role is to explain and apply, and their views are always accountable to the evidence ([see here](article.html?id=45)).
