The question comes from Quran 6:114, where Muhammad asks why he should seek a judge other than God. Ibn Kathir and Ibn Ashur relate the verse to a dispute between Muhammad and the people he addresses, in which God's revelation supplies the judgment.

Now compare the same language elsewhere. Quran 4:35 names human arbitrators in a family dispute, 5:95 calls for two fair people to make an assessment, and 4:58 instructs people to judge fairly. Those tasks sit within God's commands.

The same distinction explains prophetic authority in Sunni thought. Quran 4:59 and 4:65 direct believers to refer disputes to Muhammad, and 4:80 connects obedience to him with obedience to God. His authority as a messenger derives from the message he was commissioned to deliver and explain.
