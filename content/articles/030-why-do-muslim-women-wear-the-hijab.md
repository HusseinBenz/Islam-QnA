The headscarf may be the most visible symbol of Islam, and the most argued about. It helps to begin where the Quran begins.

## Modesty starts with men

Before the Quran says a word about women's dress, it addresses men:

> *"Tell the believing men to lower their gaze and guard their chastity. That is purer for them."* — Quran 24:30

Only in the next verse does it ask believing women to do the same, and to draw their head-coverings over their chests (24:31). Another verse asks them to wear their outer garments so that they will "be recognised and not be harmed" (33:59).

So the hijab is one half of a shared ethic. Men are responsible for their eyes and their conduct whatever a woman is wearing.

## What it means to the women who wear it

For a practising Muslim woman, the hijab is first an act of worship, like prayer or fasting. She wears it for God, and not for her father or husband.

Many women also describe a second meaning. In a culture that constantly grades women by their appearance, covering is a way of saying: deal with my mind and my character. Some call it the opposite of oppression, a freedom from being judged as a body.

It is not new or foreign either. Mary, the mother of Jesus, is shown covered in almost every image ever made of her. Christian nuns cover their hair as a sign of devotion, and the New Testament asks women to cover their heads in worship (1 Corinthians 11:5–6). Few people look at a nun and see a victim.

## Is it oppression?

It can be made into one. If a woman is forced, threatened or shamed into covering, a wrong is being done to her, and Islam does not teach it. Faith in Islam is valid only when it is chosen: "There is no compulsion in religion" (2:256).

The same principle applies in the other direction. When a government forbids a woman from covering her hair at school or at work, that is also compulsion. Respecting women means letting them decide.

When Muslim women are actually asked, the picture differs from the stereotype. John Esposito and Dalia Mogahed report in *Who Speaks for Islam?* that Muslim women surveyed by Gallup across many countries did not regard their religion as the obstacle to their progress, and that most wanted both their rights and their faith.

## It has never meant staying behind

Women in hijab are surgeons, judges, professors, engineers and athletes. Tawakkol Karman received the Nobel Peace Prize in 2011, and Ibtihaj Muhammad won an Olympic medal for the United States in 2016, both wearing it. In the first Muslim community, Aisha was among the greatest scholars of her generation, and men travelled to learn from her.

## The heart of it

The hijab is a piece of cloth. What gives it meaning is the intention behind it: a woman's decision that her relationship with her Creator matters more to her than anyone's opinion of her appearance. One may agree or disagree with her, but that decision deserves respect.
