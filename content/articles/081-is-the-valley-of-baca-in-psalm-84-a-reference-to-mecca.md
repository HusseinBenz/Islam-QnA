The Qur'an gives Mecca an older name in one verse:

> *"Indeed, the first House established for mankind was that at Bakka, blessed and a guidance for the worlds."* (Quran 3:96)

Ibn Kathir and other commentators explain that *Bakka* is a name for Mecca, or for the area of the Ka'ba within it. Psalm 84 in the Bible contains a phrase that sounds strikingly similar.

## The psalm

Psalm 84 is a song of longing for God's house, sung by pilgrims. The key verses read (84:6–8, following the New American Bible):

> *"Blessed are those whose strength is in you, in whose heart are pilgrim roads. As they pass through the Baca valley, they find spring water to drink. The early rain covers it with blessings. They will go from strength to strength and see the God of gods on Zion."*

The Hebrew *'emeq ha-bakha'* is variously translated. Some versions keep the name "Baca." Others translate it as "the valley of weeping" or "the valley of balsam trees," since *bakha'* can be related to weeping or to a kind of balsam shrub. Arabic Bible translations differ in the same way.

## Why the link has been proposed

Munqidh al-Saqqar, in *Hal Bashsharat al-Kutub bi-Muhammad?*, and earlier Muslim writers point to several features:

- **The name.** *Baca* and *Bakka* are very close in sound.
- **Pilgrimage.** The psalm describes pilgrims travelling to God's house, and pilgrimage to the Ka'ba is ancient.
- **Water in a dry valley.** The pilgrims find a spring in an arid valley. Muslims think of the well of Zamzam, which, according to Islamic tradition, sprang up for Hagar and Ishmael in the barren valley of Mecca.
- **Balsam.** Al-Saqqar quotes an Arabic Bible encyclopedia describing the *bakha'* as a balsam-like tree, and notes that such plants are associated with the Hejaz region.

One Arabic translation renders the phrase in a way that some writers read as evoking the running between Safa and Marwa.

## What the psalm itself says

A fair assessment also has to look at the geography inside the passage:

- The pilgrims' journey **ends at Zion**: *"they will see the God of gods on Zion"* (84:8). In the Hebrew Bible, Zion is Jerusalem.
- The psalm opens with longing for *"your dwelling, O LORD of hosts"* and *"the courts of the LORD"* (84:2–3), which in the context of the Psalms is the Temple.
- The notes in the New American Bible take the valley to be **probably on the way to Jerusalem**.

So on its own terms the psalm describes pilgrims passing through a dry valley on their way to Jerusalem. The variety of translations reflects a difficult Hebrew word, not hidden certainty. A similarity in sound alone cannot locate the valley.

## Weighing the proposal

The Baca–Bakka link is best treated as an **interesting possibility**, not as one of the strong arguments. Muslims do not need it. The Qur'an's claim about Mecca rests on its own authority and on the [evidence for Muhammad's prophethood](article.html?id=19), not on this psalm. Overstating weaker arguments can also make stronger ones look weak.

A sound approach is to set the proposal beside the alternatives, identify what each depends on (the name, the geography, the translation), and keep confidence in proportion to the evidence. See also [the Advocate in John](article.html?id=80) and [Mount Paran](article.html?id=82).
