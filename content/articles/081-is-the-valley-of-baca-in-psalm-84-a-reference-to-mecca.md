Psalm 84 describes pilgrims passing through a valley and finding water. Some translations preserve the Hebrew term as “Baca,” while Arabic versions cited in the book render it with images of weeping, dryness or balsam. Al-Sayyid notes its resemblance to “Bakkah” in Qur’an 3:96 and cites a reference work on a kind of balsam associated with Mecca. He also notes an interpretation of one Arabic translation as evoking the movement between Ṣafā and Marwa.

Those parallels help explain the proposed reading. To identify a place, we also need to check the geography named within the passage. Psalm 84 ends the pilgrims’ journey at Zion, and the New American Bible’s notes understand the valley as probably on the way to Jerusalem. Differences in translation can reflect a difficult Hebrew word. A similarity in sound alone cannot locate the valley.
