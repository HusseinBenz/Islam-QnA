Put two cases side by side. A daughter who inherits alongside a son ordinarily receives half of his share in their portion. A lone daughter of a woman who leaves a husband is assigned half of the estate, while the husband is assigned a quarter. Both examples come from the same passage of the Quran.

The Quran also assigns each parent a fixed sixth when the deceased leaves children. In the situation described in Quran 4:12, maternal half siblings share equally, regardless of sex. The outcome changes with the set of surviving relatives.

The book reads the two-to-one case alongside other legal obligations. A husband owes his wife a marriage gift and ongoing support even if she is wealthy. Classical jurists treated inherited property as hers to keep. That is the rationale the author offers for considering rights and obligations together when evaluating a particular share.
