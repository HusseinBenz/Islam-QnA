The first word revealed of the Quran was a command:

> *"Read, in the name of your Lord who created."* — Quran 96:1

Within two centuries, the people who received that command had built the most advanced scientific culture on earth. The story is told by the physicist Jim Al-Khalili in *The House of Wisdom*, and its traces are still in the words we use.

## A faith that asked for knowledge

The Quran asks, "Are those who know equal to those who do not know?" (39:9). The Prophet ﷺ said, "Seeking knowledge is an obligation upon every Muslim" (Sunan Ibn Majah 224), and "God has not sent down a disease without sending down a cure for it" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5678), a sentence that turned medicine into a search with a guaranteed result.

Daily worship itself demanded science. Prayer times required astronomy, the direction of Mecca required geometry and geography, and the Quran's detailed inheritance shares required mathematics.

## Names worth knowing

- **Al-Khwarizmi** (d. about 850) wrote the book whose title, *al-Jabr*, gave us the word **algebra**. The word **algorithm** is his own name in Latin form.
- **Ibn al-Haytham** (d. about 1040) showed in his *Book of Optics* that we see because light enters the eye, and insisted that claims be tested by experiment. He is often described as a pioneer of the scientific method.
- **Ibn Sina** (Avicenna, d. 1037) wrote *The Canon of Medicine*, which was used as a textbook in European universities for some five hundred years.
- **Al-Zahrawi** (d. 1013) of Córdoba described and drew some two hundred surgical instruments, several of which are recognisable in operating theatres today.
- **Al-Razi** (d. about 925) was the first to distinguish clearly between smallpox and measles.
- **Al-Biruni** (d. about 1050) calculated the radius of the earth to within a small margin of the modern figure.
- **Fatima al-Fihri** founded the mosque-school of al-Qarawiyyin in Fez in 859. It is often cited as the oldest continuously operating university in the world, and it was established by a Muslim woman.

## Institutions as well as individuals

Baghdad's House of Wisdom gathered scholars of many faiths to translate and extend the learning of Greece, Persia and India. Cities such as Baghdad, Damascus and Cairo had public hospitals that treated patients free of charge, with separate wards, pharmacies and medical teaching.

The system of numerals we use today, with its zero, reached Europe from India through the Muslim world, which is why we still call them **Arabic numerals**. Words such as *alkali*, *zenith*, *azimuth* and star names such as *Altair*, *Aldebaran* and *Betelgeuse* came by the same road.

## Why this matters

This history answers a common assumption: that faith and reason must be enemies. For the scholars above, studying creation was a way of knowing the Creator. The Quran praises those who "reflect on the creation of the heavens and the earth" and conclude, "Our Lord, You did not create this without purpose" (3:191).

The later decline of science in Muslim lands had many causes, including invasion, political division and economic change. It was not caused by taking the Quran seriously. When Muslims took it most seriously, they led the world in learning.
