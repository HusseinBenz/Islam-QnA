Stand on a beach at sunset and watch. The sun appears to sink into the sea. Everyone who says "the sun went down into the ocean" knows that it did not. They are describing what they see, and ordinary speech does this constantly.

## What the verse says

The Qur'an tells of a righteous ruler, Dhu al-Qarnayn, who travelled to the far west:

> *"Until, when he reached the setting place of the sun, he **found** it setting in a spring of dark mud, and he found near it a people."* (Quran 18:86)

> ﴿حَتَّىٰ إِذَا بَلَغَ مَغْرِبَ الشَّمْسِ وَجَدَهَا تَغْرُبُ فِي عَيْنٍ حَمِئَةٍ﴾

The key word is *wajadahā*, "he found it." The verse does not say "the sun sets in a muddy spring." It says that **this is how he found it**: what he saw from the place he reached. The description sits within his field of view.

## What the classical commentators said

This is not a modern reinterpretation invented to avoid embarrassment. Commentators writing many centuries ago said the same:

- **Ibn Kathir** explains that he saw the sun as though it were setting into the ocean, as anyone who goes to the coast sees it seem to set into the sea. In reality it does not leave its orbit.
- **Al-Qurtubi** records the explanation that the sun is far larger than the earth and could not physically enter a spring. The expression describes what appears to the eye.
- **Al-Baydawi** and the authors of ***Tafsir al-Jalalayn*** give similar readings: the spring is where the sun appeared to him to set.

Some of these scholars also accepted parts of pre-modern astronomy that are now known to be wrong. That makes their point about this verse more significant, not less. Even within the science of their own day they knew that the sun is enormous and distant, and they read the verse as describing appearance. The reading was available, and standard, long before anyone could point to a telescope.

## The Qur'an's own description of the sun

Reading 18:86 as a claim that the sun physically enters a pool would also contradict how the Qur'an describes the sun elsewhere:

> *"It is not for the sun to overtake the moon, nor does the night outstrip the day. Each is swimming in an orbit."* (Quran 36:40)

> *"It is He who created the night and the day and the sun and the moon, each swimming in an orbit."* (Quran 21:33)

A text that repeatedly places the sun in its own orbit is not claiming that it drops into a spring on earth. The careful reading is the one the commentators gave.

## A general principle

Every language describes the world from the standpoint of the observer. We still say "sunrise" and "sunset," and the stars "come out." Reading such language as a technical claim about astronomy misunderstands how human speech works. When a verse uses a verb of perception, such as *found*, *saw* or *appeared*, it is worth asking whose perception is being described.
