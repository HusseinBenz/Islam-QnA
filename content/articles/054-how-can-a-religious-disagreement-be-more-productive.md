**Before the conversation.** Read or listen to the other person's account. Check that you understand its key terms and strongest reasons. Ask where your evidence overlaps. If you disagree on whether to accept observation, reason, testimony, or a particular text, resolve what you can about that first.

**During the conversation.** State the precise disagreement. Test both sides for consistency and ask what follows if each premise is true. For instance, the philosophical argument that anything that *begins* needs a cause differs from the claim that anything that *exists* needs a cause. The first is the premise Al-Sayyid uses when asked who made God. Ask claimants to show their evidence and people quoting others to verify the quotation. A page in al-Tabari's history tells you where he recorded an account, leaving its chain and credibility to be examined.

**Read the whole body of evidence.** Quran 3:93 appeals to the Torah in a dispute about food. The scope of that appeal matters when someone uses it to support every Christian doctrine. Quran 28:56 attributes guidance to God and Quran 18:29 addresses human choice. Read both when asking about agency. Similarly, weigh Quran 16:89 on the Quran's guidance together with its commands to obey Muhammad.
