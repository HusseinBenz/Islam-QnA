question: Can a hadith with limited transmission be reliable?
summary: Yes. A report with limited transmission can be strong or weak. Its weight depends on its transmitters, the quality of its chain, and any independent support, as well as the question it is being used to answer.

You may be sure of a birth or a death because a few trusted people reported it and the details agree. Al-Sayyid uses examples like these to explain why the number of people who pass on news gives only part of the picture.

**What does āḥād mean?** In hadith terminology, it covers reports below the level of mass transmission (*tawātur*). Several transmitters can be involved. The book recalls Muhammad sending individual messengers to teach communities. It argues that some sound reports of limited transmission, supported by their circumstances, can produce certainty and others can give strong grounds for judgment.

**What about Quranic warnings against conjecture?** The Arabic word *ẓann* takes its meaning from context. Quran 53:28 criticizes unfounded claims. In verses such as 2:45–46, commentators understand the word as conviction about meeting God. The Quran's use of witness testimony gives another example of drawing a judgment from a limited number of credible people.

Scholars distinguish the evidence that justifies acting from the evidence that establishes certainty. The book's point is that each report has to be assessed on its own.
