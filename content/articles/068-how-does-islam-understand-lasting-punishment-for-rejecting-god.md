Few teachings trouble people more than the idea that some will be punished in the Hereafter without end. It is worth looking carefully at what the Qur'an actually says, the principles it places around the warning, and how Muslim scholars have reasoned about it.

## The warning

The Qur'an describes enduring punishment for those who persist in rejecting God and doing wrong:

> *"Those who disbelieved and did wrong, God will not forgive them, nor guide them to any path except the path of Hell, abiding in it forever."* (Quran 4:168–169)

It does not soften this. But it never presents it in isolation.

## Three principles around it

**1. No punishment before the message arrives.**

> *"We would never punish until We had sent a messenger."* (Quran 17:15)

> *"Messengers bringing good news and warnings, so that people would have no argument against God after the messengers."* (Quran 4:165)

What a person was given to understand, and what they did with it, matter to their judgment. Someone who never received the message, or received only a distorted version of it, is not in the position of someone who saw the truth and turned away. See [people who never heard of Islam](article.html?id=23).

**2. Mercy outweighs wrath.** The Prophet ﷺ reported that God wrote, before creation: *"My mercy prevails over My wrath"* (Sahih al-Bukhari 7404). He said God made mercy in a hundred parts, kept ninety-nine with Himself and sent one into the world, by which all creatures show compassion to one another (Sahih al-Bukhari 6000; Sahih Muslim 2752). The Qur'an says: *"My mercy encompasses all things"* (7:156), and *"God does not wrong even an atom's weight"* (4:40).

**3. The door stays open.**

> *"Say: O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of the mercy of God. Indeed, God forgives all sins. He is the Forgiving, the Merciful. And turn back to your Lord."* (Quran 39:53–54)

No one is ever told that it is too late while they are alive.

## Why lasting?

The deepest objection is proportion. How can a finite life deserve an unending result? Classical scholars gave several answers.

- **The permanence reflects the person, not only the time.** The Qur'an says of those who reject the truth: *"Even if they were sent back, they would return to what they were forbidden"* (6:28). Ibn Kathir explains that their wish to return and believe is not sincere. Many scholars drew from the verse that the punishment answers a settled disposition of the soul, not a passing act. The rejection would continue as long as the person continued.
- **The gravity of what is rejected.** Rejecting the Creator, the source of every good one has received, after clear evidence, is not one wrong among others. It is a rejection of the relationship from which all moral obligation flows. The Qur'an describes some who *"denied the signs, though their own souls were convinced of them, out of injustice and arrogance"* (27:14).
- **Eternity cuts both ways.** The same logic that makes reward unending for a few decades of faith makes the consequence of rejection lasting. Both reflect what a person chose to become.

Muslim scholars also discussed whether Hell itself would ever end. Ibn al-Qayyim examines in *Shifa' al-'Alil* the arguments of those who held that its punishment might eventually cease. The great majority of Sunni scholars held that it is lasting for those who die rejecting God after the truth was clear to them. **Muslims who enter Hell for their sins are taken out**: *"Whoever says there is no god but God and has in his heart faith the weight of a barley grain will come out of the Fire"* (Sahih al-Bukhari 44).

## Not ours to assign

In Islamic teaching, assigning a final outcome to a particular person requires knowledge of their heart, of what reached them and of their last moments, and that knowledge belongs to God alone. Muslims affirm the warning as a principle and leave individuals to God. The Qur'an tells the Prophet himself: *"Not for you is the decision at all, whether He forgives them or punishes them"* (3:128).

The question of [decree and responsibility](article.html?id=67) is closely related, since a punishment is only just if the choice was real.
