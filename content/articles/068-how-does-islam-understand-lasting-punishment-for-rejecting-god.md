The book lists the question of lasting punishment on p. 68. Its discussion on pp. 95–96 develops a related issue: why a person can be accountable for rejecting belief when God has decreed what happens. It answers that decisions are genuine and have consequences within the divine decree.

The Qur’an describes enduring punishment in passages such as 4:168–169 and says God does not punish until a messenger has been sent (17:15). It also calls people to turn back to God and speaks of his forgiveness (39:53–54). Together these passages give context to the warning. They make what a person was given to understand and what they did with it relevant to judgment.

The question of whether everlasting punishment is proportionate goes beyond the book’s discussion of free choice. In the Islamic account, assigning a final outcome to a particular person requires knowledge that belongs to God.
