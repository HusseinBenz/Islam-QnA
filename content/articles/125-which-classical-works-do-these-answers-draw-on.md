Every answer on this site ends with references. They point to the texts under discussion and to the scholars who explained them, so that you can check a claim yourself rather than take it on trust ([see how](article.html?id=49)). This guide groups the main works by field. Each one also appears on the [bookshelf](library.html), with the questions that cite it.

## How to follow a reference

- **Qur'an** references give chapter and verse, for example *2:256*. Any copy of the Qur'an, or quran.com, will take you there.
- **Hadith** references give the collection and number, for example *Sahih al-Bukhari 3015*. Numbers follow sunnah.com, where most can be read in Arabic and English. Some collections are numbered differently in other editions.
- **Classical books** are cited by volume and page in a named edition where possible, for example *Ibn Qudama, al-Mughni, 12:309 (al-Turki ed.)*. If your edition differs, look for the commentary on the same verse or the chapter on the same subject.

## The Qur'an and its commentators

The answers quote the Qur'an directly and read it through the classical commentaries:

- **Al-Tabari**, *Jami' al-Bayan*: the foundational commentary, collecting the early explanations with their chains.
- **Ibn Kathir**, *Tafsir al-Qur'an al-'Azim*: the most widely read commentary based on transmitted explanation.
- **Al-Qurtubi**, *al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an*: strong on legal questions.
- **Fakhr al-Din al-Razi**, *Mafatih al-Ghayb*: a theologian's commentary that engages rational objections.
- **Al-Baydawi**, *Tafsir al-Jalalayn*, **al-Alusi** and **al-Shawkani**: concise and later commentaries.
- **Ibn 'Ashur**, *al-Tahrir wa-l-Tanwir*; **al-Sa'di**, *Taysir al-Karim al-Rahman*; **al-Shinqiti**, *Adwa' al-Bayan* and *Daf' Iham al-Idtirab*: modern commentaries.

## Hadith and its criticism

- **The collections:** *Sahih al-Bukhari*, *Sahih Muslim*, the *Sunan* of Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah, the *Musnad* of Ahmad, and earlier works such as the *Musannaf* of 'Abd al-Razzaq and the *Musnad* collections of al-Tayalisi and al-Bazzar.
- **Commentaries:** Ibn Hajar's *Fath al-Bari*, al-Nawawi's commentary on Muslim, Ibn Battal's commentary on al-Bukhari, and Ibn 'Abd al-Barr's *al-Tamhid*.
- **Method and transmitters:** Ibn al-Salah's handbook of hadith sciences, al-'Ala'i's *Jami' al-Tahsil*, al-'Uqayli's *al-Du'afa'*, al-Dhahabi's *Siyar A'lam al-Nubala'*, and al-Khatib al-Baghdadi's *Taqyid al-'Ilm* on early writing.
- **Difficult reports:** al-Shafi'i's *Ikhtilaf al-Hadith*, Ibn Qutayba's *Ta'wil Mukhtalif al-Hadith* and al-Tahawi's *Sharh Mushkil al-Athar*.

## Creed, reason and prophethood

- **Ibn Taymiyya:** *Dar' Ta'arud al-'Aql wa-l-Naql* on reason and revelation, *al-Jawab al-Sahih* on Christianity and prophethood, *al-Nubuwwat*, and his collected fatwas, *Majmu' al-Fatawa*.
- **Al-Ghazali:** *al-Iqtisad fi al-I'tiqad* and *al-Munqidh min al-Dalal*.
- **Ibn al-Qayyim:** *Shifa' al-'Alil* on decree and wisdom, *al-Da' wa-l-Dawa'* on prayer and sin, and *al-Sawa'iq al-Mursala*.
- **Ibn Abi al-'Izz**, commentary on the creed of al-Tahawi. **Al-Bayhaqi**, *Dala'il al-Nubuwwa*. **Ibn Hazm**, *al-Fasl*.

## Legal theory and law

- **Legal theory:** al-Shafi'i's *Jima' al-'Ilm*, Ibn Hazm's *al-Ihkam*, al-Juwayni's *al-Burhan*, al-Ghazali's *al-Mustasfa*, Abu Ya'la's *al-'Udda*, al-Mardawi's *al-Tahbir*, the Taymiyya family's *al-Musawwada*, and al-Shatibi's *al-Muwafaqat* and *al-I'tisam*.
- **Law:** Ibn Qudama's *al-Mughni*, Ibn al-Mundhir's *al-Ijma'*, and Ibn al-Qayyim's *I'lam al-Muwaqqi'in* and *Ahkam Ahl al-Dhimma*.

## History, biography and language

- **Biography of the Prophet:** Ibn Hisham's edition of Ibn Ishaq, and Ibn Sa'd's *al-Tabaqat al-Kubra*.
- **History:** al-Tabari's *History*, Ibn Kathir's *al-Bidaya wa-l-Nihaya*, and Ibn Khaldun's *Muqaddima*.
- **Companions:** Ibn 'Abd al-Barr's *al-Isti'ab*.
- **Arabic:** Ibn 'Aqil's commentary on the *Alfiyya*, and Ibn Faris's *al-Sahibi*.

## Modern studies

Some answers also cite modern scholarship. Examples are Muhammad Abdullah Draz on the Qur'an and its ethics, Muhammad Mustafa al-A'zami on early hadith writing, Mustafa al-Siba'i on the Sunnah, Rahmatullah Kairanawi and Munqidh al-Saqqar on the Bible, and scientific sources where a question involves science. Further reading listed under an answer is offered for those who want to go deeper. It is not a substitute for the primary texts.

## A word of caution

References show where a claim can be checked. They do not replace the work of reading the passage in context. Where the answers describe a scholarly disagreement, the references usually point to more than one side. Readers who want to study a subject seriously should do so with a qualified teacher ([see here](article.html?id=44)).
