Read the opening chapter of the Qur’an alongside the Throne Verse and chapter 112. These short passages bring together God’s mercy, sovereignty, knowledge and oneness. Al-Sayyid sees this picture of God as one reason to take Muhammad’s claim seriously.

He also points to the breadth of teachings conveyed during a ministry traditionally understood to have lasted twenty-three years. Muhammad preached, led a community and dealt with migration, family responsibilities and conflict. The content transmitted from that period has continued to shape Islamic law and everyday ethical teaching. For the author, the argument depends on the qualities of those teachings and how they hold together. The number of books written about them is evidence of their reach.

A reader can examine an example directly, such as how Qur’anic and hadith texts treat care for relatives and neighbors. Muslim scholars have collected and discussed such conduct in works including al-Bukhari’s *Al-Adab al-Mufrad* and Muhammad Abdullah Draz’s study of Qur’anic ethics.
