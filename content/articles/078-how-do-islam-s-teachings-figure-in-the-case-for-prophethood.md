A claim to speak for God can be tested in part by **what is said**. A false prophet might perform tricks, but it is much harder to sustain, over two decades, a body of teaching that is consistent, profound and good. Classical Muslim scholars such as Ibn Taymiyya, in *al-Nubuwwat*, argued that the content of a prophet's message is one of the strongest proofs of his truthfulness.

## A picture of God

Start with three short passages that Muslims recite constantly:

- **Al-Fatiha** (1:1–7): God as Lord of all worlds, the Merciful, Master of the Day of Judgment, the One alone who is worshipped and asked for help.
- **The Throne Verse** (2:255): *"God, there is no god but He, the Living, the Self-Sustaining. Neither drowsiness overtakes Him nor sleep... His Throne extends over the heavens and the earth, and preserving them does not tire Him."*
- **Surat al-Ikhlas** (112): *"Say: He is God, One. God, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, nor is there to Him any equivalent."*

Together they present God as merciful and sovereign, knowing and one. He is utterly transcendent, yet near to those who call on Him. This picture is coherent, and it is free of the crude features of many ancient conceptions of the divine.

## A complete way of life, in twenty-three years

Muhammad's mission lasted roughly twenty-three years. During that time he preached in Mecca under persecution, migrated to Medina, built and led a community, made treaties, fought wars, judged disputes, and lived as a husband, father and neighbour. Out of that period came:

- a complete form of worship, including prayer, fasting, almsgiving and pilgrimage;
- detailed law on marriage, inheritance, trade, contracts, crime and war;
- an ethics of everyday life that is still taught in Muslim homes.

The point is not the *number* of books written about these teachings, although that is huge. It is their **quality and coherence**. They hold together across many areas of life and have guided communities in very different places and centuries.

## Ja'far's summary

When the early Muslims fled persecution to Abyssinia, the Christian king asked them what this new religion was. Ja'far ibn Abi Talib answered:

> *"O King, we were a people of ignorance. We worshipped idols, ate carrion, committed indecencies, cut off our relatives, mistreated our neighbours, and the strong among us devoured the weak. Then God sent us a messenger from among us, whose lineage, truthfulness, trustworthiness and chastity we knew. He called us to God, to worship Him alone. He commanded us to speak the truth, fulfil trusts, keep ties of kinship, be good to neighbours, and refrain from forbidden things and bloodshed."* (Musnad Ahmad 1740, narrated by Umm Salama)

## Test an example yourself

Take one theme, such as care for neighbours and relatives, and read the sources directly:

- *"Gabriel kept urging me to treat the neighbour well until I thought he would make him an heir"* (Sahih al-Bukhari 6014).
- *"Whoever wishes to have his provision expanded and his life extended, let him maintain ties of kinship"* (Sahih al-Bukhari 5986).
- *"Worship God and associate nothing with Him, and be good to parents, relatives, orphans, the needy, the near neighbour and the distant neighbour, the companion at your side, the traveller..."* (Quran 4:36).

Al-Bukhari gathered hundreds of such reports in *al-Adab al-Mufrad*, his book on manners and character. Muhammad Abdullah Draz's *Dustur al-Akhlaq fi al-Qur'an* (*The Moral World of the Qur'an*) shows how the Qur'an's moral teaching forms a system rather than a list of rules.

## How this fits the larger case

Good teaching does not by itself prove revelation. A wise human being could teach good things. But the case for prophethood is cumulative. A teaching of this depth, delivered by an unlettered man under extreme pressure, consistent with [his own conduct](article.html?id=74) and joined to [a text no one could imitate](article.html?id=75), is hard to explain as the work of either a deceiver or a deluded man.
