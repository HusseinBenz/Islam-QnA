Becoming a Muslim is simpler than most people expect.

## The testimony of faith

A person enters Islam by saying, with understanding and sincere belief:

> **Ash-hadu an la ilaha illa Allah, wa ash-hadu anna Muhammadan rasul Allah.**
>
> *"I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, and I bear witness that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah."*

This is called the *shahada*. The first half means that nothing deserves worship except the one Creator. The second means accepting Muhammad ﷺ as His final messenger, and following the guidance he brought.

There is no baptism, no committee and no required ceremony. Saying it in front of other Muslims is good, because it gives you witnesses and a community, but what makes you a Muslim is your belief and your word before God.

## What you are accepting

Along with the testimony comes belief in God, His angels, His revealed books, all His messengers, the Day of Judgment, and His decree. You do not turn your back on the earlier prophets. A Muslim believes in Abraham, Moses and Jesus:

> *"We make no distinction between any of them, and to Him we have submitted."* — Quran 2:136

Many people who embrace Islam describe it as a return to something they always knew. Islam teaches that every person is born with a natural recognition of God.

## Your past is forgiven

When 'Amr ibn al-'As came to accept Islam, he hesitated, worried about everything he had done. The Prophet ﷺ said to him: "Do you not know that Islam wipes away what came before it?" (Sahih Muslim 121)

You begin with a clean record:

> *"Say to those who have disbelieved: if they cease, what has previously occurred will be forgiven for them."* — Quran 8:38

## What comes next

1. **Wash.** It is the established practice to take a full bath or shower on entering Islam.
2. **Learn the prayer.** The five daily prayers are the centre of a Muslim's life. Learn them at your own pace.
3. **Find people.** Visit a mosque, and look for knowledgeable, balanced and kind Muslims who can teach you.
4. **Read.** Begin with the Quran in a good translation, and a biography of the Prophet ﷺ.

Take it step by step. The Quran itself was revealed gradually over twenty-three years. Aisha explained that the first passages revealed were about faith and the life to come, and that the detailed rules came only once hearts had settled. She said that if the first command had been "do not drink wine", people would have refused (Sahih al-Bukhari 4993).

## Common worries

- **"I am not good enough yet."** Islam is where improvement starts. Come as you are.
- **"Must I change my name?"** No, unless its meaning contradicts the faith.
- **"What about my family?"** Islam commands you to keep treating your parents with love and respect, even if they do not share your belief (Quran 31:15).
- **"What if I make mistakes?"** You will, as every Muslim does. The door of forgiveness is always open.

## A last word

Muhammad Asad, who tells his own story in *The Road to Mecca*, described his conversion as coming home. If your heart has recognised the truth, you do not need anyone's permission to answer it.

> *"This day I have perfected for you your religion, completed My favour upon you, and have approved for you Islam as your religion."* — Quran 5:3
