Muslims believe Islam is the truth, and they are glad to share it. That conviction sits alongside clear teachings about how people of other religions are to be treated.

## Every human being is honoured

> *"We have certainly honoured the children of Adam."* — Quran 17:70

The honour is given to the children of Adam, which means every human being. The Quran also teaches that religious diversity is part of God's plan for this life, and not something to be ended by force:

> *"Had your Lord willed, everyone on earth would have believed, all of them together. Would you then compel people until they become believers?"* — Quran 10:99

## The rule: justice and kindness

> *"Allah does not forbid you from being kind and just toward those who have not fought you because of religion nor driven you from your homes. Indeed, Allah loves those who are just."* — Quran 60:8

The Arabic word used here for kindness, *birr*, is the word used for devotion to one's own parents.

## People of the Scripture

Jews and Christians have a particular closeness. A Muslim may eat their food, and a Muslim man may marry a Jewish or Christian woman without her changing her religion (5:5). Marriage in the Quran is built on "affection and mercy" (30:21), so the Quran is permitting the closest of human bonds across the line of faith. In debate, Muslims are told: "Do not argue with the People of the Scripture except in the best manner" (29:46).

## How the Prophet ﷺ lived it

- In Medina he concluded a charter with the Jewish tribes that recognised them as one community alongside the believers, each keeping its own religion.
- A delegation of Christians from Najran visited him, and the early biographies record that they performed their prayers in his mosque.
- He warned: "Whoever wrongs a person under covenant, or diminishes his right, or burdens him beyond his capacity, or takes anything from him without his consent, I will be his opponent on the Day of Resurrection" (Sunan Abi Dawud 3052).
- He said that anyone who kills a person under treaty "will not smell the fragrance of Paradise" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3166).
- He traded with his Jewish neighbours until the end of his life (Sahih al-Bukhari 2916).

## The historical record

When the caliph Umar entered Jerusalem in about 637, he gave its Christians a written guarantee of safety for their lives, churches and crosses. In Muslim Spain, Jews, Christians and Muslims built together a culture that María Rosa Menocal describes in *The Ornament of the World*. When Jews were expelled from Spain in 1492, it was the Ottoman sultan who gave them refuge. Ancient churches and synagogues still stand across the Middle East after fourteen centuries of Muslim rule. Thomas Arnold documents this history at length in *The Preaching of Islam*.

## What about "do not take them as allies"?

Verses such as 5:51 are sometimes quoted to claim that Muslims may not befriend others. The word used, *awliya'*, means protectors and political patrons, and the verses were revealed about siding with hostile powers in time of war. The Quran itself draws the line in 60:8–9: kindness and justice to those who live in peace, and no alliance with those who wage war on the faith. The Prophet's own practice, described above, shows how it was understood.

> *"To you your religion, and to me mine."* — Quran 109:6
