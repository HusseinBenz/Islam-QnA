People often imagine that Islam condemns everyone born in the wrong place or time. It does not. The question is settled by a principle the Quran states plainly:

> *"We never punish until We have sent a messenger."* — Quran 17:15

## God's justice is exact

> *"Indeed, Allah does not wrong anyone, even by the weight of an atom."* — Quran 4:40

> *"Allah does not burden a soul beyond what it can bear."* — Quran 2:286

Messengers were sent, the Quran says, "so that people would have no argument against Allah" (4:165). It follows that a person whom no message reached still has their argument, and God, who is more just than any of us, will honour it.

## Who is excused

Muslim scholars describe several kinds of people who are not held to account for rejecting Islam, because they never had a real chance to accept it:

- those who lived and died without hearing of it at all;
- those who were unable to understand it, such as young children and people without the mental capacity;
- those who heard only a distorted picture.

The third group matters a great deal today. Imam al-Ghazali, writing nine hundred years ago in *Faysal al-Tafriqa*, divided the non-Muslims of his time into three: those who never heard the Prophet's name, who are excused; those who heard of him truthfully, with the evidence, and turned away, who are responsible; and those who heard his name only alongside lies about him. He judged this last group to be like the first, and he expressed the hope that God's mercy would embrace most of them.

A person today who knows of Islam only through frightening headlines has not yet met it.

Hadith reported by Imam Ahmad and others also describe people who could not receive the message in this life, such as the deaf or those who lived between prophets, being given their own fair test on the Day of Judgment.

## What does make a person accountable

The Arabic word *kufr*, usually translated "disbelief", literally means *to cover*. It describes someone who recognises the truth and covers it, out of pride, habit, or fear of what people will say. That is a choice, and God judges choices.

## Leave the verdict to God

Muslims are not permitted to assign individuals to Paradise or Hell. Only God knows what reached a person, what they understood, and what lay in their heart. The Prophet ﷺ told of a woman of ill repute who was forgiven because she climbed down a well to bring water to a dog dying of thirst (Sahih al-Bukhari 3467). God's mercy reaches further than our guesses.

## And for the one reading this

The question changes once the message arrives. If you are reading these words, you have heard something of Islam. You are not asked to accept it without thought. You are asked only to be honest: to look into it sincerely and follow the truth where you find it.

> *"Whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it."* — Quran 99:7
