Read side by side, two consecutive verses can look inconsistent:

> *"If good comes to them, they say, 'This is from God,' and if evil befalls them, they say, 'This is from you [Muhammad].' Say: **All is from God** (kullun min 'indi Allah). So what is the matter with these people that they can hardly understand any statement?"* (Quran 4:78)

> *"Whatever good befalls you is from God, and whatever evil befalls you is **from yourself**. And We have sent you to the people as a messenger, and sufficient is God as Witness."* (Quran 4:79)

One verse says everything is from God. The next says evil is from yourself. Is this a contradiction?

## The context: an accusation against the Prophet

Verse 78 answers a specific accusation. Some people in Medina credited prosperity to God, but blamed every hardship on Muhammad's presence. It was a familiar move, and the Qur'an says Pharaoh's people did the same with Moses (7:131). The reply denies that the Prophet is the source of misfortune: **all of it, good and bad, happens within God's decree.** Nothing occurs outside His knowledge and will.

## Two different questions

Verse 79 then turns to a different question. It does not ask *where events come from*. It asks *who deserves credit and who bears responsibility*.

- **Good** is God's gift and favour. No one earns it so completely that it becomes his own achievement.
- **Harm** often follows from what people themselves do. Another verse states this connection explicitly: *"Whatever misfortune befalls you is because of what your hands have earned, and He pardons much"* (42:30).

So verse 78 speaks at the level of **decree**: everything is created and permitted by God. Verse 79 speaks at the level of **cause and responsibility**: human choices bring consequences, and people should look first to their own conduct. Al-Tabari, Ibn Kathir and Muhammad al-Amin al-Shinqiti, in his short work on apparently conflicting verses, *Daf' Iham al-Idtirab*, all explain the pair in this way.

## The Arabic marks the difference

The two verses do not even use the same phrase. Verse 78 says *min 'indi Allah*, "from God's presence," meaning by His decree. Verse 79 says of good *fa-mina Allah*, "it is from God," meaning by His grace, and of evil *fa-min nafsika*, "from yourself," meaning by your own doing. The change of wording signals the change of question.

## An example

Think of a drought. Within the religious frame, the drought happens within God's decree, as verse 78 says. People can still be responsible when their decisions make the resulting shortage worse, through hoarding, waste or injustice, as verse 79 says. Both statements are true at once. They describe the same event at different levels.

This also has a limit worth stating. Verse 79 does not give anyone licence to treat every affliction as a judgment on the person who suffers it. The Prophet ﷺ himself was the most severely tested of people, and illness and loss strike the righteous too ([see here](article.html?id=65)).

## How to read apparent contradictions

The Qur'an itself invites close reading on exactly this point:

> *"Do they not reflect upon the Qur'an? Had it been from other than God, they would have found in it much contradiction."* (Quran 4:82)

Note that this verse comes only three verses later. When two passages seem to conflict, check:

1. **The Arabic**: are the same words actually used?
2. **The context**: what question is each answering?
3. **Related passages**: what do other verses on the subject say?
4. **Scope**: is each statement general, or about a particular case?
