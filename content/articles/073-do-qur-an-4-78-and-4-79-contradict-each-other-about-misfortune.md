question: Do Qur’an 4:78 and 4:79 contradict each other about misfortune?

The first verse answers people who credited prosperity to God but blamed hardship on Muhammad. It places both kinds of event within God’s decree. The next verse speaks about who deserves credit or bears responsibility. Good is described as a gift from God, and harm can follow from what people do. Qur’an 42:30 makes this connection to human actions explicit. The book reads the second verse’s address as applying beyond Muhammad.

Consider a drought. Within the religious frame, what happens remains within God’s decree. People can still be responsible when their decisions make the resulting shortage worse. The second explanation works at a different level from the first, and it gives us no basis to read every affliction as a judgment on a particular sufferer.

For other apparent contradictions, the book recommends checking the Arabic, the context, and related passages, along with whether each statement is general or speaks to a particular case. Qur’an 4:82 itself invites close reading.
