The image of Muslims offering "Islam or the sword" is centuries old. It does not survive a look at the Quran or at the map.

## Faith cannot be forced

> *"There is no compulsion in religion. The right path has become distinct from the wrong."* — Quran 2:256

> *"Say: The truth is from your Lord. So whoever wills, let him believe, and whoever wills, let him disbelieve."* — Quran 18:29

In Islam, faith is an act of the heart. A declaration made under threat is worth nothing before God, and forcing one is a sin.

## Look at the map

- **Indonesia** has the largest Muslim population of any country. No Muslim army ever conquered it. Islam arrived with traders and teachers.
- The same is true of **Malaysia**, much of **East and West Africa**, and **Central Asia's** steppe peoples.
- **India** was ruled by Muslim dynasties for centuries and remained overwhelmingly Hindu.
- **Egypt, Syria, Iraq and Lebanon** still have ancient Christian communities, fourteen centuries after Muslim rule began. Where conversion happened in these lands, historians find that it took place gradually over several hundred years.
- Today Islam continues to grow in **Europe and the Americas**, where Muslims hold no power at all.

## The conquerors who converted

In 1258 the Mongols destroyed Baghdad and killed the caliph. Muslims were the defeated party, with no sword to offer. Within a few decades the Mongol rulers of Persia and Central Asia had themselves embraced Islam. A conquering people adopted the religion of those it had conquered.

## What historians have said

The British orientalist De Lacy O'Leary wrote:

> *"History makes it clear, however, that the legend of fanatical Muslims sweeping through the world and forcing Islam at the point of the sword upon conquered races is one of the most fantastically absurd myths that historians have ever repeated."* — *Islam at the Cross Roads* (1923)

Thomas Arnold's *The Preaching of Islam* (1896) remains the classic study. Working through region after region, he shows that Islam spread chiefly through merchants, teachers and the example of ordinary believers.

Mahatma Gandhi, after reading the Prophet's life, wrote:

> *"I became more than ever convinced that it was not the sword that won a place for Islam in those days in the scheme of life. It was the rigid simplicity, the utter self-effacement of the Prophet, the scrupulous regard for his pledges."* — *Young India* (1924)

## An honest distinction

Muslim states did fight wars and expand, as every empire of that age did, and Muslim rulers were not always just. But the expansion of a state's borders is one thing, and the conversion of hearts is another. Under Muslim rule, Jews and Christians kept their religions, their houses of worship and their own courts.

A faith imposed by force is abandoned as soon as the force is removed. Islam has outlived every empire that carried its name, and it is still growing.
