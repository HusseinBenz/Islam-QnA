Many Muslims feel either that they must win every argument about Islam, or that they should avoid such conversations entirely. The Qur'an describes a third way. Explain clearly and answer honestly, then leave the outcome to God.

## Answering is part of the prophetic task

The Qur'an does not avoid objections. It quotes them and responds. When opponents asked why the Qur'an was not revealed all at once, it answered: *"Thus [it is], that We may strengthen your heart thereby, and We have recited it in distinct stages"* (25:32). It then adds a general promise: *"They do not bring you any argument except that We bring you the truth and the best explanation"* (25:33).

It also states its disagreements plainly. On the claim that God has a son, it says: *"They say, 'The Most Merciful has taken a son.' You have done an atrocious thing"* (19:88–89), and then explains why such a claim is unfitting for God (19:92–93).

A few verses later in Surat al-Furqan comes this instruction:

> *"So do not obey the disbelievers, and strive against them with it a great striving."* (Quran 25:52)

Al-Tabari records that Ibn 'Abbas explained "with it" as *with the Qur'an*. This verse was revealed in Mecca, before any fighting was permitted. The "great striving" it commands is the effort of argument, explanation and proclamation through revelation. Answering questions well is part of that effort.

## Accuracy: say what you know, and say whose view it is

- **Start with the main point**, not a side issue.
- **Distinguish teaching from opinion.** Say when something is agreed upon by Muslim scholars, when it is one school's position, and when it is your own understanding.
- **Do not overclaim.** It is better to say "I don't know, but I'll find out" than to invent an answer. The Prophet ﷺ warned: *"Whoever lies about me deliberately, let him take his seat in the Fire"* (Sahih al-Bukhari 110).
- **Convey even a little.** *"Convey from me, even if it is one verse"* (Sahih al-Bukhari 3461). You do not need to be a scholar to share what you know accurately.

## Manner: gentleness is not optional

> *"It was by mercy from God that you were gentle with them. Had you been harsh and hard-hearted, they would have scattered from around you."* (Quran 3:159)

The Prophet ﷺ said: *"God is gentle and loves gentleness, and He gives for gentleness what He does not give for harshness"* (Sahih Muslim 2593). He told those he sent out to teach: *"Make things easy and do not make them difficult; give good news and do not drive people away"* (Sahih al-Bukhari 69).

The quality of the exchange is itself a form of explanation. Someone trying to understand Islam may learn as much from how you speak as from what you say.

## Responsibility, and its limits

> *"The Messenger's duty is only to convey."* (Quran 5:99)

> *"So remind, for you are only a reminder. You are not a controller over them."* (Quran 88:21–22)

> *"You do not guide whom you love, but God guides whom He wills."* (Quran 28:56)

You are responsible for your **effort, accuracy and conduct**. You are not responsible for the other person's response. That is between them and God, and the Qur'an insists that *"there is no compulsion in religion"* (2:256). Knowing this removes a great deal of pressure. It lets you listen properly, admit what you do not know, and part on good terms even without agreement.
