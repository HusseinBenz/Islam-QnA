Older sources may discuss the question you encountered for the first time today. The author gives several examples.

- **How can God know what people will do and still judge them?** In the book’s account, [people make choices for which they are accountable](article.html?id=67&lang=en), even within God’s knowledge and decree. The Quran depicts Iblis, an adversary of humanity, as choosing to rebel and later tempting people. The episode illustrates the role of choice and testing in discussions of [why God permits harm or wrongdoing](article.html?id=65&lang=en).
- **Could the reported sorcery have affected Muhammad’s revelation?** The early reports describe a personal affliction. Al-Nawawi records Qadi ‘Iyad’s reading that the effects described concerned Muhammad’s bodily condition and private life, while the delivery of revelation remained protected.
- **Why do reports about the Dajjal, a figure associated with the end times, describe different eyes as impaired?** One version describes the right eye and another the left. Qadi ‘Iyad reads the Arabic description as capable of referring to different kinds of impairment, so the reports could describe different features.

Choose a person who will hear the whole question and can show where their answer comes from. A clear answer may be a reading, a conversation, or an honest admission that there is disagreement. Keep track of what is established and where you would like more evidence. A parent or teacher can help by listening before explaining and making it safe to ask again.
