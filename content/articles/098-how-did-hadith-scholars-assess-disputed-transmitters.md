Consider the claim that Muhammad told Abu Hurayra to visit less often because he kept coming for food. There are two claims here, the saying and the suggested motive. Al-Sayyid notes that al-Bazzar and al-ʿUqayli found no sound prophetic chain for the saying. The food explanation has even less support as a reason for those words.

This illustrates *al-jarḥ wa-al-taʿdīl*, the discipline of recording and weighing evidence for or against a transmitter's reliability. Critics asked whether an allegation had a sound basis and whether it mattered to the accuracy or honesty of that person's reporting.

The collections of al-Bukhari and Muslim include transmitters from several theological currents, including people with Shiʿi, Kharijite or Qadarite views. The author uses that breadth to illustrate how scholars could recognize a transmitter's reliability across disagreements. Actual judgments also depended on the person and the report.
