At first glance the rule looks unfair. A closer look shows a system designed around responsibility.

## First, what came before

In pre-Islamic Arabia women did not inherit at all. They could even be inherited themselves, passed on with the estate, a practice the Quran abolished (4:19). Then came a verse that changed the law of the world's inheritance:

> *"For men is a share of what parents and close relatives leave, and for women is a share of what parents and close relatives leave, be it little or much, an obligatory share."* — Quran 4:7

This was the seventh century. In England, a married woman gained the right to own property in her own name only with the Married Women's Property Acts of 1870 and 1882.

## The rule and its reason

The Quran says: "Allah instructs you concerning your children: for the male, the equivalent of the share of two females" (4:11). The reason lies in what each is then required to do with the money.

**The son:**

- must pay a dowry (*mahr*) to his bride when he marries (4:4);
- must house, feed and clothe his wife and children, whatever her own wealth (4:34);
- is responsible for supporting his parents, and unmarried sisters, if they are in need.

**The daughter:**

- *receives* a dowry when she marries;
- is supported by her husband, and before that by her father or brother;
- keeps her inheritance, earnings and property entirely as her own. No one, including her husband, has any claim on it.

Picture a brother who inherits 20,000 and a sister who inherits 10,000. He must spend his on a dowry and then on a household for decades. She keeps all of hers, and receives a dowry in addition. After a few years, she may well hold more of the family's wealth than he does.

## It is not a rule about gender

If Islam valued women at half, the rule would apply in every case. It does not:

- When someone dies leaving children and both parents, **the mother and the father each receive one sixth**, the same share (4:11).
- Half-brothers and half-sisters through the mother **share equally** (4:12).
- There are cases in the detailed law where a woman inherits more than a man, and cases where she inherits while a male relative in the same family receives nothing.

The shares are set by closeness to the deceased, by generation, and by financial burden.

## The larger picture

Islamic law balances rights against duties. In money matters, men are given more obligations, and so in some cases a larger share to meet them. A woman is given complete financial independence and a guaranteed right to be supported. A system should be judged whole, and not by one clause read alone.

> *"Men shall have a share of what they have earned, and women shall have a share of what they have earned."* — Quran 4:32
