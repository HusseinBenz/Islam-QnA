## One family

> *"O mankind, We created you from a male and a female, and made you into peoples and tribes so that you may know one another. Indeed, the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous of you."* — Quran 49:13

In one sentence this verse removes every ground on which racism stands. We have one origin. Our differences exist so that we may *know* one another, and not so that we may despise one another. Rank belongs to character alone, which only God can fully judge.

The Quran goes further and treats human variety as something beautiful:

> *"And among His signs is the creation of the heavens and the earth, and the diversity of your languages and your colours."* — Quran 30:22

## The first racist

In the Quran's account, the first being to claim superiority by origin was Satan. Ordered to honour Adam, he refused, saying: "I am better than him. You created me from fire and him from clay" (7:12). Pride in what one is made of is, in Islam, the original sin of the devil.

## The Prophet's final word

In his farewell sermon, before the largest crowd of his life, the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ said:

> *"O people, your Lord is one and your father is one. An Arab has no superiority over a non-Arab, nor a non-Arab over an Arab; a white person has no superiority over a black person, nor a black person over a white person, except by righteousness."* — Musnad Ahmad 23489

He said this in seventh-century Arabia, where tribe and lineage were everything.

## He practised it

- **Bilal**, an Abyssinian and a former slave, was chosen to give the first call to prayer. On the day Mecca was opened, it was Bilal who climbed onto the Ka'ba to call it.
- **Salman** from Persia and **Suhayb**, known as "the Roman", were among his closest Companions.
- When Abu Dharr insulted a man by referring to his mother, the Prophet ﷺ told him: "You are a man who still has some of the age of ignorance in him" (Sahih al-Bukhari 30).

## Built into worship

Five times a day Muslims stand for prayer in straight rows, shoulder to shoulder: the employer beside the cleaner, the prince beside the labourer. Once a year, on the pilgrimage, millions of people of every colour wear the same two white sheets and stand on the same plain.

In 1964 that sight changed Malcolm X. He had believed racism was beyond cure. From Mecca he wrote home about eating from the same plate and praying beside people whose skin was "the whitest of white", and concluded:

> *"America needs to understand Islam, because this is the one religion that erases from its society the race problem."* — *The Autobiography of Malcolm X*

## An honest word

Muslims are human, and racism can be found among Muslims, as it can among any people. When it appears, it contradicts the religion.

Today the followers of Islam include every nation on earth. Arabs are only about a fifth of them. A faith that began in one desert valley has become a home for all of humanity, as its scripture said it would.
