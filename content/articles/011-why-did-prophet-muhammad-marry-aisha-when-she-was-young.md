Few questions about the Prophet's life are raised as often as this one, and it deserves a careful answer that separates what the sources say, how they have been interpreted, and what follows for today.

## What the reports say

The most widely cited reports are narrated by Aisha herself. She says the Prophet ﷺ contracted marriage with her when she was six, and that she joined his household when she was nine (Sahih al-Bukhari 5134; Sahih Muslim 1422). The reports appear in the two most trusted Sunni collections, through more than one chain.

Aisha also describes the interval between the contract and the beginning of married life. She was playing on a swing with her friends when her mother came to prepare her (Sahih al-Bukhari 3894). She continued to play with dolls in the Prophet's house, and her friends came to play with her, which he welcomed (Sahih al-Bukhari 6130).

## The modern alternative chronology

Some modern writers argue that Aisha was older, perhaps in her late teens. They reason indirectly from other dates, for example the reported age gap between Aisha and her sister Asma', or Aisha's memories of events in Mecca. Those arguments rely on estimates that are themselves uncertain. The age reports, by contrast, are direct statements attributed to Aisha.

To weigh the two approaches, ask which dates are independently established and which are derived from approximations. Most classical and many modern scholars give more weight to the direct report. Fahd al-Ghufayli's *al-Sana al-Wahhaj fi Sinn 'A'isha 'ind al-Zawaj* discusses the arguments in detail.

## The historical setting

The marriage took place in seventh-century Arabia. Three features of the sources are worth noting.

**No recorded objection.** The Prophet's enemies attacked him relentlessly: they called him a poet, a madman and a sorcerer, and they spread a [slander about Aisha herself](article.html?id=74) (Sahih al-Bukhari 2661). The surviving sources record no contemporary objection to her age. That tells us something about the norms of the time. It cannot tell us everything about the range of private opinion, or about what she experienced.

**Physical maturity.** Classical jurists did not treat a contract as permission for married life with a child who was not physically ready. Al-Nawawi, commenting on Sahih Muslim 1422, records that a girl is not to be given to her husband until she is fit for it, and that this is judged by her physical condition, not by a fixed age. Ibn Hajar discusses the same report in *Fath al-Bari*.

**Aisha's own voice.** Aisha remembered her married life with affection, became one of the greatest scholars among the Companions, and narrated over two thousand hadith. Many of the details of the Prophet's domestic life come from her. Her own accounts are the main source for this question.

Marriage customs have varied across places and periods. In medieval Europe, for example, canon law set the minimum age of marriage for girls at twelve. A seventh-century practice should be understood in its context before it is judged by the standards of another era.

## What follows for today

Historical context explains a practice. It does not settle what is right today. For decisions about marriage now, three considerations are distinct and each matters:

- **Maturity**, physical, emotional and intellectual;
- **Free and informed consent**: the Prophet ﷺ said a woman's permission must be sought before her marriage (Sahih al-Bukhari 5136);
- **Health**: the World Health Organization documents the serious risks of pregnancy in adolescence.

Islamic law gives significant weight to preventing harm, and many Muslim-majority countries today set a minimum age of marriage by law. See also [what Islamic teaching says about women's rights and duties](article.html?id=90).
