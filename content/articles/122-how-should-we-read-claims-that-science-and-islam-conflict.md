In 1887, Albert Michelson and Edward Morley looked for a measurable effect predicted by one leading model of the ether, a proposed medium for light. The expected effect was far smaller than the model predicted. Special relativity later explained the behavior of light without requiring such a medium. Research on atoms also revealed limits to classical physics. The earlier equations still work well within many familiar conditions.

Al-Sayyid uses examples like these to show how measurements can uncover limits in a highly successful theory. He holds that a sound reading of reliable revelation is compatible with well-established empirical findings. That is a theological position. Testing any specific claim of conflict still takes work on both sides.

1. **Name the claim.** What did the study observe, and what is the proposed explanation?
2. **Read the passage in context.** How certain are its wording, transmission, and interpretation?
3. **Compare the two.** Do they make incompatible claims about the same thing? If the evidence is incomplete, leave the question open.
