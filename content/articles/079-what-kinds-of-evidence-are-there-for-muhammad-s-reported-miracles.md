The examples include a sound from a palm trunk Muhammad used to lean against, trees moving to give him privacy, food proving sufficient for a large group and water flowing between his fingers. These accounts appear in hadith collections and works such as al-Bayhaqi’s *Signs of Prophethood*.

Al-Sayyid’s argument concerns **convergence**. Different reports may support a shared proposition even when they tell of different events. Hadith scholars examine who transmitted each report, how the accounts connect to earlier witnesses, and where separate lines share a source. This is a kind of historical inquiry through testimony. It can help a reader weigh how early a report is and how well it is attested.

An extraordinary explanation raises an additional question about how to interpret the testimony. The book places the reports alongside the Qur’an, Muhammad’s character and his teachings in a cumulative theological case.
