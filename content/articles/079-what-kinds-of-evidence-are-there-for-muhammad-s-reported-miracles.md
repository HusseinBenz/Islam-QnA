The Qur'an's central sign is the Qur'an itself. When the Meccans demanded spectacular miracles, it answered: *"Is it not sufficient for them that We have sent down to you the Book which is recited to them?"* (29:51). But the hadith collections also preserve many reports of other signs. What kind of evidence are they, and how should they be weighed?

## Some of the reports

- **The crying palm trunk.** The Prophet ﷺ used to lean against a palm trunk while preaching. When a pulpit was built and he moved to it, the trunk made a sound like a crying child, and it quietened only when he came down and placed his hand on it (Sahih al-Bukhari 3584, and other routes).
- **Water from his fingers.** At Hudaybiya, when the people had almost no water, he placed his hand in a vessel and water flowed from between his fingers, enough for about fifteen hundred people to drink and make ablution (Sahih al-Bukhari 3576).
- **Food that sufficed.** During the digging of the trench around Medina, Jabir prepared a small meal. The Prophet invited the whole army of diggers, and the food fed them all with some left over (Sahih al-Bukhari 4101).
- **Trees that moved.** Jabir describes the Prophet seeking privacy in an open valley, and two trees coming together to screen him, then returning to their places (Sahih Muslim 3012).
- **The splitting of the moon.** *"The Hour has drawn near, and the moon has split"* (Quran 54:1). Anas reports that the Meccans asked for a sign and were shown the moon split in two (Sahih al-Bukhari 3637).

Al-Bayhaqi gathered these and many other reports, with their chains, in *Dala'il al-Nubuwwa* (*The Signs of Prophethood*). Ibn Taymiyya devoted a large part of *al-Jawab al-Sahih* to them.

## How the reports are evaluated

These are **historical claims transmitted by testimony**, and hadith scholars assess them with the same tools they use for any report ([see here](article.html?id=101)):

- **Who transmitted each report**, and were they known to be reliable?
- **Is the chain connected** back to an eyewitness?
- **Do separate chains share a source**, or are they independent?
- **Do the versions agree** on the core event?

The reports vary in strength. Some are recorded by al-Bukhari and Muslim through several Companions. Others are weaker.

## Convergence

The key argument is **convergence**. Even if one doubted any single report, many different reports, from different Companions, describing different events, all support one shared proposition: **extraordinary signs occurred at the Prophet's hands.** Hadith scholars call this *tawatur ma'nawi*, mass transmission in meaning. The individual details may each be reported by few, but the common core is reported by so many independent witnesses that collusion or collective error becomes implausible.

Compare how we know that a famous general was brave. No single anecdote proves it, but dozens of independent accounts of different battles leave little room for doubt.

## The extra question

An extraordinary claim raises a further question: how to interpret the testimony. Someone who is certain in advance that miracles are impossible will reject every report, however strong. That is a philosophical position, not a historical finding. If God exists and sends prophets ([see the arguments](article.html?id=58)), signs confirming a prophet are not absurd. They are what we might expect.

For that reason the reports are best placed alongside the other evidence in a cumulative case: [the Qur'an and its challenge](article.html?id=75), [the Prophet's character](article.html?id=74), [his teachings](article.html?id=78) and [fulfilled prophecies](article.html?id=76). Each strand strengthens the others.
