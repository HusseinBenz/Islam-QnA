Quran 2:256 says there is no compulsion in religion. A report in Sahih al-Bukhari has traditionally been read as prescribing death for apostasy, understood here as leaving Islam. Ibn Qudama records agreement on the general rule among the jurists he discusses.

**How did interpreters put the texts together?** Al-Sayyid argues that early scholars knew and interpreted both sources. He cites al-Tabari, who read the Quranic verse in light of the groups historically allowed to continue in their religion under the legal arrangement of *jizya*. Al-Tabari distinguished them from people he placed in different legal categories, including apostasy. Ibn Kathir records a similar reading of the verse regarding Jewish and Christian communities.

**Why is this still debated?** Readers raise freedom of conscience, tolerance, and episodes from Muhammad's life. Al-Sayyid understands the dispute in part as a difference over the place of divine commands and individual autonomy. He calls for calm, close reading of the sources and cautions that labeling the rule a *ḥadd*, or fixed penalty, can obscure a legal question about its classification. Explaining the classical interpretation helps clarify the disagreement over the texts and the reach of historical state authority.
