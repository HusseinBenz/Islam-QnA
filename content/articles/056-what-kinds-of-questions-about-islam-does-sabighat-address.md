**Foundations.** Questions about God include first causes, cosmic laws, evolution, multiverse hypotheses, quantum physics, the purpose of worship, suffering, prayer, judgment, and freedom under divine decree. Quran questions ask about its origin and alleged errors in language, claims about nature, or consistency. Questions about Muhammad include what supports prophethood and how to understand reports about his marriages to Aisha and Safiyyah, polygyny, the Banu Qurayzah episode, and the ‘Uraynah group. Legal questions include women’s rights, war, slavery, apostasy, and prescribed penalties.

**Sources and interpretation.** Questions about the Sunnah ask what authority prophetic teachings have, what weight to give a report with limited transmission, how the sources were collected, how scholars assessed their transmitters, and what to do when a report seems to conflict with scripture, reason, observation, science, or another report. Other topics include how Muslims interpret texts, whether a consensus can be established, what authority the first generation has, and how jurists understand the penalties called *hudud*.

Al-Sayyid presents this as a practical survey of questions he encountered. Some subjects occur in both groups. For example, someone interested in a penalty may ask about the morality of the law or about how its source and interpretation are established. The [source guide](article.html?id=125&lang=en) lists the works in the book’s bibliography.
