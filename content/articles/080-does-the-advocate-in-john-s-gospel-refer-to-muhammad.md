The Qur'an says that earlier scripture foretold Muhammad:

> *"And when Jesus son of Mary said: O Children of Israel, I am the messenger of God to you, confirming what came before me of the Torah, and bringing good news of a messenger to come after me, whose name is Ahmad."* (Quran 61:6)

> *"Those who follow the Messenger, the unlettered prophet, whom they find written with them in the Torah and the Gospel."* (Quran 7:157)

Muslims have long looked to John's Gospel, chapters 14 to 16, where Jesus promises his disciples that after he leaves, a figure called the *paraklētos* will come. The Greek word is usually translated "Advocate," "Helper," "Counsellor" or "Comforter."

## The passage

Key lines from John (New American Bible translation):

- *"I will ask the Father, and he will give you **another** Advocate to be with you always"* (14:16).
- *"It is better for you that I go. For if I do not go, the Advocate will not come to you"* (16:7).
- *"When he comes, the Spirit of truth, he will guide you to all truth. He will not speak on his own, but he will speak what he hears, and will declare to you the things that are coming"* (16:13).
- *"He will glorify me"* (16:14).

## Why Muslims have read it as Muhammad

The connection is old. Ibn Ishaq's biography of the Prophet, as preserved by Ibn Hisham, already quotes a Syriac-Palestinian version of John 15 in which the coming figure is called *al-Munhamanna*. Ibn Ishaq explains it as "Muhammad" in Syriac and *al-baraqlitus* in Greek. Ibn Taymiyya in *al-Jawab al-Sahih* and Rahmatullah Kairanawi in *Izhar al-Haqq* developed the argument at length. The main points are these:

- **"Another" Advocate.** The first epistle of John calls Jesus himself a *paraklētos* (1 John 2:1). "Another" suggests someone of the same kind as Jesus, a human messenger.
- **"He will not speak on his own, but will speak what he hears."** This closely matches the Qur'an's description of Muhammad: *"Nor does he speak from desire. It is only a revelation revealed"* (53:3–4).
- **"He will declare the things that are coming."** The Qur'an and hadith contain many statements about the future.
- **"He will glorify me."** The Qur'an honours Jesus as a great prophet, born of a virgin, the Messiah, and clears his mother of slander.
- **"If I do not go, he will not come."** Muslims ask why the Holy Spirit, which the Gospels describe as already active before and during Jesus' ministry (for example Luke 1:15, 1:41; John 20:22), would need Jesus to depart before coming.

## The Christian reading, stated fairly

A fair comparison must also state what the passage says that supports the traditional Christian reading:

- John 14:26 explicitly identifies him: *"The Advocate, the **holy Spirit** that the Father will send in my name."*
- John 14:17 calls him *"the Spirit of truth, which the world cannot accept, since it neither sees nor knows it. But you know it, because it remains with you, and will be in you."*
- Christians understand the promise as fulfilled at Pentecost (Acts 2), within weeks of Jesus' departure.

Muslim writers respond that "Spirit" is used in the Bible for inspired human messengers too (1 John 4:1–3 tells believers to "test the spirits"), and that some phrases may reflect later interpretation. Readers can weigh these arguments.

## A claim to avoid

Some popular writers claim that the original Greek word was *periklytos* ("much praised," close in meaning to "Ahmad"), and that it was later changed to *paraklētos*. **No known Greek manuscript of John has *periklytos*.** A claim about the original wording of a text needs manuscript evidence, and this one has none. The stronger Muslim arguments rest on the content of the passage, not on an unattested reading.

## How to compare

Read both passages in full and in context. Separate what follows from the text itself from what depends on a wider interpretation, in both directions. Then ask which reading accounts for more of the passage with fewer assumptions. See also [is Muhammad mentioned in the Bible?](article.html?id=21)
