The Qur’an describes Jesus as announcing a messenger named Ahmad (61:6) and speaks of an unlettered messenger described in earlier scripture (7:157). Al-Sayyid compares this with John 16, where Jesus speaks of a future Advocate who will convey what he hears and announce things to come. A Muslim reader may see a parallel with how the Qur’an describes revelation to Muhammad (53:3–4 and 75:18).

John 14:26 calls the Advocate the Holy Spirit, and John 16 speaks of the Spirit of truth. That context is central to the Christian reading of the passage. The Greek word transmitted in John is *paraklētos*, commonly translated “Advocate,” “Helper” or “Comforter.” The proposal cited by al-Sayyid that a word meaning “much praised” originally stood there is a different textual claim and would need manuscript evidence.

For a reader comparing the two traditions, the strong starting point is to read both passages in context and identify which conclusions follow from the text and which depend on a wider interpretation.
