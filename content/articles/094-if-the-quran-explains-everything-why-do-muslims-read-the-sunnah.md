The objection runs like this. The Qur'an says it explains everything, so anything outside it, including the hadith, is unnecessary. The objection rests on two verses. Read each one through to the end before deciding what it claims.

## Verse one: "We have neglected nothing in the Book" (6:38)

> *"There is no creature on earth, nor bird that flies with its wings, but they are communities like you. We have not neglected anything in the Book. Then to their Lord they will be gathered."* (Quran 6:38)

The verse is about animals and birds, God's knowledge of them, and their return to Him. Many commentators, as al-Tabari records, understood "the Book" here as **God's comprehensive record of creation**, often called the Preserved Tablet. Compare: *"There is no creature on earth but that upon God is its provision, and He knows its place of dwelling and of storage. All is in a clear register"* (11:6).

Even if one reads "the Book" here as the Qur'an, the question remains *how* the Qur'an guides. That leads to the second verse.

## Verse two: "a clarification of all things" (16:89)

> *"And We have sent down to you the Book as a clarification of all things, and guidance and mercy and good tidings for the Muslims."* (Quran 16:89)

Al-Baydawi, al-Alusi and al-Shawkani all explain this as a clarification of **all things of religion**, the guidance people need. Some of it is stated in detail. Some is given by **directing people to other sources**: to the Messenger, and, in the scholars' account, to recognised forms of reasoning such as consensus and analogy that the Qur'an and Sunnah authorise.

A doctor's handbook can "cover everything" partly by telling you which specialist to see. That is not a weakness of the handbook. It is how it works.

## What the Qur'an says the Prophet is for

The same chapter, a few verses earlier, defines the Prophet's role:

> *"And We sent down to you the Reminder so that you may **make clear to the people** what was sent down to them, and that they might reflect."* (Quran 16:44)

The Qur'an then repeatedly makes the Prophet's guidance binding:

> *"Whatever the Messenger gives you, take it; and whatever he forbids you, refrain from it."* (Quran 59:7)

> *"Whoever obeys the Messenger has obeyed God."* (Quran 4:80)

> *"Nor does he speak from desire. It is only a revelation revealed."* (Quran 53:3–4)

> *"Whoever opposes the Messenger after guidance has become clear to him and follows other than the way of the believers..."* (Quran 4:115)

## A Companion's answer

The Companion 'Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud once quoted the Prophet's curse on certain cosmetic practices. A woman objected that she had read the whole Qur'an and had not found it there. He replied: *"If you had read it, you would have found it. Have you not read: 'Whatever the Messenger gives you, take it, and whatever he forbids you, refrain from it'?"* She said she had. He said: *"Then he forbade it"* (Sahih al-Bukhari 4886; Sahih Muslim 2125). The Qur'an contains the Sunnah's authority, even where it does not contain every detail.

## The practical test: prayer

The Qur'an commands prayer dozens of times. It does not say how many daily prayers there are, how many units each has, what to recite, or exactly when each begins and ends. Every Muslim community, from the earliest generation on, has learned these from the Prophet's practice: *"Pray as you have seen me pray"* (Sahih al-Bukhari 631). The same is true of the amounts of zakat and the rites of pilgrimage. Without the Sunnah, many of the Qur'an's own commands cannot be carried out.
