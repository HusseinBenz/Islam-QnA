The objection usually starts from two verses that use the word “Book.” Read each one through to the end before deciding what it is claiming.

- **“We have omitted nothing from the Book” (6:38).** The verse speaks of animals and birds as communities and describes their return to God. Al-Sayyid reads “the Book” here as God's comprehensive record, often called the Preserved Tablet, and compares it with 11:6. Even if a reader takes “the Book” to mean the Quran, the question of how it guides still remains.
- **“An explanation of all things” (16:89).** Al-Baydawi, al-Alusi and al-Shawkani understand this as guidance in matters of religion. Some guidance comes as detailed statements. Some comes as directions to follow the Messenger or, in their accounts, through other recognized ways of reasoning such as analogy and scholarly consensus.

On that reading, the Quran can give comprehensive guidance by identifying further sources of guidance. The next question is which teachings can actually be traced to Muhammad.
