Some thoughts about faith are **questions**. You are curious or puzzled, and an answer helps. Others are **intrusions**. They arrive uninvited, often during prayer or at quiet moments, feel urgent and disturbing, and come back however many times you answer them. The two can feel similar, but they need different responses.

## The Companions experienced this too

Intrusive thoughts about faith are not new, and they are not a sign that something is wrong with your belief. Some of the Prophet's Companions came to him and said: *"We find in ourselves thoughts that each of us considers too terrible to speak of."* He asked, *"Have you really experienced that?"* They said yes. He replied: *"That is clear faith"* (Sahih Muslim 132).

The scholars explained this, as al-Nawawi records in his commentary on Muslim. The **distress** a believer feels at such a thought, and the sense that it is too terrible to say, are evidence of faith, not of its absence. A person who did not care would not be troubled.

Another report describes a man who said he had thoughts he would rather be burned to ashes than speak. The Prophet ﷺ said: *"Praise be to God, who has reduced his plot to mere whispering"* (Sunan Abi Dawud 5112).

## Passing thoughts are not sins

The Prophet ﷺ said: *"God has pardoned my community for what their souls whisper to them, so long as they do not act on it or speak of it"* (Sahih al-Bukhari 5269; Sahih Muslim 127). A thought that arrives against your will says little about what you believe or want. You are not accountable for it.

## The prophetic response: refuge, and stop

The Prophet ﷺ described a recurring chain of questions: *"Satan comes to one of you and says, 'Who created this? Who created that?' until he says, 'Who created your Lord?' When he reaches that, let him seek refuge in God and stop"* (Sahih al-Bukhari 3276; Sahih Muslim 134).

Note what this guidance does **not** say. It does not say the question has no answer (it does: see [who created God?](article.html?id=60)). It says that when a question has become a **loop**, the remedy is to step out of it, not to answer it yet again.

When 'Uthman ibn Abi al-'As complained that distracting thoughts confused his prayer and recitation, the Prophet ﷺ told him to seek refuge in God from them and to spit lightly to his left three times. 'Uthman said he did so, and God took it away from him (Sahih Muslim 2203).

## How to tell which you have

| A question | An intrusive thought |
|---|---|
| You want to understand something | You want the anxiety to go away |
| An answer brings lasting relief | An answer brings relief for minutes, then the urge to check again |
| You can put it aside and return later | It feels urgent and hard to set aside |
| It came from a book, conversation or observation | It seems to arrive from nowhere, often at quiet or sacred moments |

**If it is a question:** name the claim, and look for a reasoned answer ([how to check a claim](article.html?id=49)).

**If it is an intrusion:** let the thought pass without arguing with it. Seek refuge in God, saying *a'udhu billahi min al-shaytan al-rajim*, and return to what you were doing. Re-checking and re-arguing tend to feed the loop.

## When to seek professional help

If intrusive thoughts or repeated rituals take up a lot of time, cause serious distress or interfere with daily life, speak to a doctor or a qualified mental-health professional. This pattern can be part of obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), which can focus on religion and is treatable. Seeking treatment is fully consistent with faith. The Prophet ﷺ said: *"God has not sent down a disease without sending down its cure"* (Sahih al-Bukhari 5678).
