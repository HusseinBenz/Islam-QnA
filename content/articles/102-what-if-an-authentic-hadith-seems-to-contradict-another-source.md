Hafsa once heard a statement about people who had pledged allegiance to Muhammad and asked how it fit with a Quranic verse. The report goes on to show Muhammad pointing her to the next verse. Al-Sayyid mentions Hafsa and Aisha to illustrate how early Muslims asked about apparent inconsistencies.

An apparent conflict may involve two Quranic passages, the Quran and a hadith, two hadiths, or a report and an argument from reason, experience or science. First identify the best-attested wording and its context. Then ask what the other source actually establishes. Two claims might concern different groups or circumstances. If interpretations still compete, compare the quality of evidence for each.

Classical legal theorists studied conflicting evidence as a distinct problem. Hadith scholars and commentators wrote on difficult and apparently conflicting reports, and modern studies have continued that work.
