The most concrete example in the book is Qur’an 30:1–6. It says that the Romans, understood here as the Byzantine Empire, will defeat their opponents within a few years after a defeat. The traditional reading connects this with the Byzantine recovery against the Sasanian Persians. To evaluate the claim historically, ask when the passage was proclaimed, which events count as the defeat and the reversal, and how the time period is reckoned.

The book also cites Qur’an 15:9, understood by Muslims as a promise of the Qur’an’s preservation, and 22:15, which the author reads as referring to God’s support for Muhammad. Those are different kinds of claims from a prediction of a military reversal. Al-Sayyid’s argument is that announcing a future result in advance exposes a claim to failure. The more specific and independently checkable the claim, the more it can contribute to the argument.
