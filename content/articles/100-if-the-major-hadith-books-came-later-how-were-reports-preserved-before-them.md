A later anthology can contain earlier material. The important historical question is how the compiler acquired it and what evidence survives of its prior circulation.

Al-Sayyid points to smaller written records, known as *ṣuḥuf*, alongside memorization, teaching, and transmission from one generation to the next. He cites Muhammad Mustafa al-Aʿzami's study tracking those records before systematic compilation. Other works study the narrower question of when writing was permitted and the broader history of how collections took shape.

Early records show that hadith writing had begun before the famous collections. A reader assessing an individual report still needs to examine its chain, wording and any other surviving versions.
