**Origin.** When something begins to exist, we normally look for a cause. The book applies that idea to the universe. The Quran asks whether we were created without cause or created ourselves. Cosmologists can trace expansion back to a hot, dense early universe. Discussion of what preceded the earliest period and of what thermodynamics can establish is distinct from the philosophical question of an absolute beginning or ultimate cause.

**Order.** Biological and physical systems exhibit patterns and can often be understood through regular processes. Al-Sayyid asks why such a world exists at all. For him, the intelligibility and complexity of what we study invites an inference to wisdom, even when we can describe the processes involved.

**Care.** The author also considers the relationship between living beings and their environment. He cites the provision of food before and after birth, plants and animals, water and land, and our capacity to understand and use what we find. He calls this an argument from providence. A reader can appreciate the observations and still ask which explanation of them is strongest.
