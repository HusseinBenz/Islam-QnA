“I know of no disagreement” tells you what a writer has found. “There was a consensus” makes a stronger claim about what all relevant scholars held. Al-Sayyid uses this distinction to explain Ahmad ibn Hanbal's sharp warning about claiming consensus.

Abu Dawud also records Ahmad appealing to agreement about Quran 7:204 and its application to prayer. A fuller version of Ahmad's warning says people may have disagreed and names Bishr al-Marisi and al-Asamm. Ibn Rajab and other commentators connect it with claims made by theologians who did not know the earlier positions well. Ibn al-Qayyim connects it with appeals to alleged consensus against sound hadith.

Abu Yaʿla reads the statement as a warning against overclaiming, especially where someone has not surveyed the early disagreements. The practical question is whether a particular consensus can be documented. Claims differ in strength even for scholars who accept the authority of a proven consensus.
