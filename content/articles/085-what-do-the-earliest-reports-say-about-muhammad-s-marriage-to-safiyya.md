question: What do the earliest reports say about Muhammad’s marriage to Safiyya?
summary: A report in al-Bukhari places the start of the marriage after the group had left Khaybar and Safiyya’s waiting period had ended. She had been taken captive in the campaign in which her previous husband was killed.

The report describes a journey away from Khaybar and says the marriage was consummated when Safiyya became eligible at Sadd al-Rawha. Another report describes Muhammad freeing her and making her freedom her marriage gift. This is the source context for the book’s answer to the claim that married life began on the day her previous husband died.

Al-Sayyid points to later interactions between Safiyya and Muhammad, including a report she transmitted about visiting him during his retreat in a mosque. The book also notes that al-Bukhari preserves a different woman’s objection when Muhammad approached her, showing that a refusal could appear in the record. Those reports give some evidence about what was preserved. They do not directly establish the circumstances of Safiyya’s decision or what choices were available to her as a wartime captive. The reported timeline and the question of consent each deserve to be read on their own evidence.
