Imagine a report that seems impossible. Is it poorly attested, hard to picture, or genuinely contradictory? Each possibility calls for a different response.

**Start with the evidence.** Al-Sayyid argues that if careful reasoning gives a believer grounds to trust a revelation, it can also give grounds to accept what that revelation teaches about things beyond ordinary observation. People may begin with different assumptions shaped by their experiences and communities. When they disagree about a hadith, a report about Muhammad, a useful first question is whether there is good evidence that he actually said it.

**Separate the unfamiliar from the impossible.** A genuine contradiction would assert that the same thing happened and did not happen in the same sense. Resurrection can be hard to imagine, but that feeling alone does not establish a contradiction. The author's conclusion rests on two steps that a reader can examine separately: the case for trusting the revelation and the case for a particular interpretation.
