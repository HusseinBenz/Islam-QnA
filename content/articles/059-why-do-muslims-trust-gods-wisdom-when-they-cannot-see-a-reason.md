If you find a part in a well-designed device and cannot tell what it does, you can investigate before deciding whether it has a purpose. Al-Sayyid uses the analogy to illustrate how established knowledge can guide judgment while some details are unknown.

His account has four steps. First, consider the reasons for believing in God. Second, consider what the order of creation suggests about God’s knowledge, power, and wisdom. Third, recognize how much human explanations can change with new information. Fourth, examine why Muslims trust the prophets. The book points to their character, consistency, and the signs they brought. That trust is why Muslims read the Quran and the established Sunnah for insight into God’s purposes.

This approach applies within Islamic belief. A reader still deciding whether those starting points are sound can work through them in order. Someone living through a loss can ask for an explanation and for care, even when the answer to why this happened remains unknown.
