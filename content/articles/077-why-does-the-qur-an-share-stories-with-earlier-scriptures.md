If two scriptures tell a similar story, that resemblance can be interpreted in more than one way. The Qur’an presents continuity with earlier prophetic teaching and also retells some episodes with different details, purposes and emphases. After accounts of Moses and Noah it addresses Muhammad directly and says the information reached him by revelation.

Al-Sayyid asks how someone traditionally described as unlettered came to deliver narratives with such detail, rhetorical structure and moral focus. He sees their overlap with earlier traditions as consistent with a shared divine source. Historical inquiry can also ask what accounts circulated orally and compare each text’s form. The presence of a parallel, by itself, cannot establish how a story reached a particular speaker.
