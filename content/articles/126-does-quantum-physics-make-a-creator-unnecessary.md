A theory can describe an event with probabilities even when it gives no definite outcome for a single measurement. Quantum experiments can then test the predicted pattern across many measurements. That is evidence about the behavior of physical systems.

When someone says a quantum event comes from “nothing,” ask what the word means in that explanation. A physical model can start from a state with defined properties and laws. That is a different starting point from the philosophical idea of absolute nothingness. Even a successful physical explanation leaves room to ask why there is a physical reality for the model to describe.

*Sabighat* lists arguments drawn from quantum physics in its overview. This distinction applies the book's later discussion of scientific models and wider claims about reality.
