Some people believe that a religious person must never question, and that a doubt is already a betrayal. The Quran paints a very different picture.

## Those who asked

- **The angels.** When God announced the creation of humankind, the angels asked: "Will You place on earth one who will cause corruption and shed blood?" (2:30). They were not rebuked. They were answered.
- **Abraham.** The father of the prophets said: "My Lord, show me how You give life to the dead." God asked, "Do you not believe?" He replied: "Yes, but so that my heart may be at rest" (2:260). God then showed him.
- **Moses** asked to see God Himself (7:143).

If the angels and the greatest of the prophets could ask, so can you. Wanting your heart to be at rest is not weakness of faith. Abraham wanted the same thing.

## What the Prophet ﷺ told people who doubted

Some of his Companions came to him deeply troubled. They said they found thoughts in their minds so terrible that they could not bear to speak them. He asked, "Do you really experience that?" They said yes. He said: **"That is clear faith"** (Sahih Muslim 132).

Their distress at the thought was the proof of their belief. An unwanted thought that passes through your mind is not a sin, and it does not make you a hypocrite.

## A book that demands thinking

The Quran does not say "believe and do not ask". Again and again it says: *Do they not reflect? Do they not use reason? Do they not look?* It challenges its opponents: "Bring your proof, if you are truthful" (2:111). It forbids following what one has no knowledge of (17:36). It even invites readers to search it for contradictions (4:82).

And it gives the method: "Ask the people of knowledge if you do not know" (16:43). The Prophet ﷺ said: "The cure for ignorance is to ask" (Sunan Abi Dawud 336).

## Two who walked through doubt

**Al-Ghazali**, perhaps the most influential scholar in Islamic history, went through a crisis in which he doubted everything, including his own senses and reason. He describes in *Deliverance from Error* how he came out of it with a faith deeper than he had before.

**Jeffrey Lang**, an American mathematics professor raised to distrust religion, expected the Quran to demand blind belief. He found that it kept raising his own objections before he could, and answering them. He named his book *Even Angels Ask*.

## How to carry a question

- **Be sincere.** There is a difference between asking in order to understand and asking in order to avoid. Only you know which you are doing.
- **Go to good sources.** A doubt picked up from a thirty-second video deserves more than a thirty-second answer.
- **Be patient.** Not every question is settled in a day. What you already know to be true does not become false because one thing is still unclear.
- **Ask God.** The prayer Muslims repeat in every unit of prayer is a request: "Guide us to the straight path" (1:6).
