Deuteronomy 33 links Sinai, Seir and Paran in Moses’ blessing. Habakkuk 3 pictures God coming from Teman and the Holy One from Paran. Genesis 21 places Ishmael in the wilderness of Paran, while Islamic tradition connects Ishmael to Mecca. Munqidh al-Saqqar joins these strands. He argues that Seir can evoke Jesus and Paran Muhammad. He takes the holy multitudes and law of Deuteronomy as signs of a messenger and his followers, and reads Habakkuk’s place names and imagery of radiance and praise as pointing to Arabia.

Several steps can be evaluated separately. A place name can recur in different locations, as al-Saqqar argues from the name Seir, and prophetic language can use the past tense. Those possibilities do not establish that the Paran in these passages is Mecca or that the holy retinue refers to people. A common Jewish and Christian reading understands the passages as poetic accounts of God’s appearance, with Paran in the region south of Israel. The passages are best compared alongside the geographical and textual evidence on which each reading depends.
