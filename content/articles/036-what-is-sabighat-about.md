The book’s title starts with a story about David. The Quran says God taught him to make armor and to set its links in proportion. Al-Sayyid uses those well-fitted links as an image for something intellectual. A reader needs evidence, sound judgment, and people with whom they can ask hard questions safely.

The book asks what leads some young people to leave Islam or question teachings such as the authority of the Prophet’s example, why familiar objections recur, and how religious educators can respond. It puts three kinds of work together. Build good habits before a question arrives, work through a question when it does, and learn how to discuss the disagreement itself. The author developed the book from courses on these questions and points readers to longer treatments.

The comparison with armor grows out of the Quran’s own replies to objections. Muhammad faced both arguments and physical hostility. In the passage behind the book’s approach, the Quran is his instrument for argument and explanation. The first step is to understand the question someone actually has.

The opening pages present the book as a starting point for general readers, with references for those who want to follow the evidence further. Al-Sayyid closes with a brief expression of confidence in Islam’s future.
