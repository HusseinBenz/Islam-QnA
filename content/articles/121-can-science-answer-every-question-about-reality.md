A physicist can use equations to model how a universe behaves. A further question is why there is a universe with those laws at all. The questions are related, and the evidence needed to answer each may differ.

*Sabighat* traces one source of the modern conflict narrative to disputes involving scientists and church authorities in Europe. The author cautions against treating that history as the template for every religion. He also argues that scientists, like other people, may bring philosophical assumptions to their work. A physicist's expertise gives weight to a claim within physics. A broader conclusion deserves to be examined on its own reasoning, regardless of who draws it.

For example, Stephen Hawking and Leonard Mlodinow argue that a physical theory can account for spontaneous creation under laws such as gravity. Al-Sayyid asks whether such an account settles the further question of why those laws and that reality exist. When reading a claim like this, look for the observation, the model, and the conclusion the author draws. Ask what evidence supports each step.
