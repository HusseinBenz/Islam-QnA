During the siege of Medina, the early Muslim accounts say the local Banu Qurayza group broke its agreement with Muhammad’s community. The Qur’an describes the fear of the besieged community and later says that some of those who supported the attacking coalition were killed and others captured. According to al-Bukhari and Muslim, the group surrendered on the condition that they accept Saʿd ibn Muʿadh’s judgment. He ruled that the fighting men be executed and the women and children taken captive. Muhammad accepted the judgment.

Al-Sayyid argues that the case involved a collective treaty breach and that the ruling distinguished the fighting group from children. Another account, attributed to a captive named Atiyya, says signs of puberty were used to decide who would be spared execution. This matters because puberty and modern age categories can diverge. The sources leave questions about the exact numbers and how the category was applied to particular captives.

The account concerns one group and a specific siege. Its severity, including captivity and the extent of collective punishment, remains part of what a reader may need to evaluate.
