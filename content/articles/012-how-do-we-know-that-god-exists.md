Islam does not ask you to believe in God blindly. The Quran speaks to the mind as much as to the heart, and it opens the question with an argument anyone can test:

> *"Or were they created by nothing, or were they the creators of themselves? Or did they create the heavens and the earth? Rather, they are not certain."* — Quran 52:35–36

## Three options, and only one that works

Everything that begins to exist has an explanation outside itself. You began to exist. So did the earth, the sun, and, according to modern cosmology, the universe itself. That leaves three possibilities:

1. **The universe came from nothing.** But nothing has no power to produce anything. From nothing, nothing comes.
2. **The universe created itself.** But to create itself it would have to exist before it existed, which is a contradiction.
3. **The universe was brought into being by something beyond it**, something that did not itself begin, and that has the power and will to create.

Almost a thousand years ago, Imam al-Ghazali put the argument in three lines: whatever begins to exist has a cause; the world began to exist; therefore the world has a cause. A chain of dependent things cannot stretch back forever, because then nothing would ever have got started. The chain must end in One who depends on nothing.

## A universe that is written, not scribbled

The second sign is order. The laws of nature are stable, mathematical and precise, and life is possible only because they are balanced within very narrow limits. The Quran invites us to look, and look again:

> *"You do not see in the creation of the Most Merciful any inconsistency. So return your vision to the sky: do you see any breaks?"* — Quran 67:3

We never assume that a book wrote itself. A universe that can be read like a book deserves the same honesty.

## The witness inside you

The third sign is closer than the stars. The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that every child is born upon the *fitrah*, a natural disposition to recognise its Creator (Sahih al-Bukhari 1358). This is why belief in a higher power appears in every culture ever studied, and why even people who deny God so often call out to Him when the plane shakes or the diagnosis arrives. The Quran describes exactly this moment (29:65).

## "Then who created God?"

The question assumes that God is one more created thing, which is precisely what God is not. The argument does not say "everything has a cause". It says everything *that begins to exist* has a cause. The Creator of time did not begin. If He needed a creator, and that one needed another, there would be no creation at all, and yet here we are.

> *"Say: He is Allah, the One. Allah, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, nor is there to Him any equivalent."* — Quran 112:1–4

## Evidence, not compulsion

God has made His existence clear enough for anyone who looks sincerely, and has left us free enough that faith remains a choice. He promises:

> *"We will show them Our signs in the horizons and within themselves until it becomes clear to them that it is the truth."* — Quran 41:53
