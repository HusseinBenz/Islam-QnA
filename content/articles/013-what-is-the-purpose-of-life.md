Every person eventually asks it, usually in a quiet moment: *why am I here?* If we are only atoms that happened to arrange themselves, then nothing we do finally matters. Something in us refuses to accept that, and the Quran says that refusal is right:

> *"Did you think that We created you without purpose, and that you would not be returned to Us?"* — Quran 23:115

## The answer in one verse

> *"I did not create jinn and mankind except to worship Me."* — Quran 51:56

Many people hear "worship" and picture only rituals. In Islam the word is far wider. Ibn Taymiyya defined worship as a comprehensive name for everything God loves and is pleased with, in words and actions, outward and inward. Prayer is worship. So is honesty in business, patience with your children, and care for your parents. The Prophet ﷺ said that even your smile in the face of your brother is charity (Jami' al-Tirmidhi 1956).

Purpose in Islam is therefore not a small religious corner of life. It is the whole of life, lived knowingly before God.

## Life as a test, not a trap

> *"He who created death and life to test you, which of you is best in deed."* — Quran 67:2

Notice the wording: *best* in deed, not *most*. The test is about sincerity and quality, and it is fitted to each person. The rich are tested by wealth and the poor by need; the strong by power and the weak by patience. No one is given a test they cannot pass:

> *"Allah does not burden a soul beyond what it can bear."* — Quran 2:286

## Why nothing else satisfies

People try to fill the place of purpose with money, status, pleasure or achievement. These are good things in their place, but none of them can carry the weight of a human life, and all of them end.

Imam al-Ghazali learned this at the height of success. He was the most celebrated professor in Baghdad when he realised that his heart was empty and that he was teaching for fame. He left it all in 1095 to search for certainty, and wrote about the journey in *Deliverance from Error*.

Nine centuries later, Muhammad Asad, an Austrian journalist, noticed the same emptiness on the faces of prosperous passengers on the Berlin underground. That evening he opened the Quran and read: "Competition in worldly increase diverts you, until you visit the graves" (102:1–2). He tells in *The Road to Mecca* how that moment settled his heart.

> *"Truly, in the remembrance of Allah do hearts find rest."* — Quran 13:28

## What this means for you

You are not an accident, and you are not forgotten. You were created deliberately, by One who knows you, for a purpose worthy of you: to know Him, to do good, and to return to Him. Everything else in Islam, from prayer to charity, is the practical shape of that one purpose.
