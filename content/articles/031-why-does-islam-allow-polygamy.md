## What Islam found, and what it changed

Before Islam, a man in Arabia, as in much of the ancient world, could marry as many women as he wished, with no obligations and no limit. The Bible describes Abraham, Jacob, David and Solomon as having more than one wife.

Islam did not start this practice. It limited it and attached conditions. The verse in question is this:

> *"Marry women of your choice, two, three or four. But if you fear that you will not be just, then only one."* — Quran 4:3

As far as is known, this is the only place in any major scripture where the words "marry only one" appear. And the Quran adds a sober warning:

> *"You will never be able to be perfectly just between wives, however much you may wish to."* — Quran 4:129

The message is that one wife is the safe norm, and that more than one is a heavy responsibility which most men should not take on.

## The circumstances of the verse

The verse was revealed after the battle of Uhud, when many Muslim men had been killed, leaving widows and orphans without protectors. It opens by speaking of justice toward orphans. Its first purpose was care for vulnerable women and children. It was not revealed to indulge men.

## The conditions

A man who marries a second wife must, by law:

- treat both wives equally in time, housing and financial support;
- be able to provide fully for both households;
- give each wife and her children full legal rights, inheritance and public recognition.

A woman also has protections. According to many jurists, she may write into her marriage contract that her husband will not take another wife, and if he breaks the condition she may end the marriage.

## Why allow it at all?

Because real life produces situations for which the alternatives are worse.

After wars, there are often far more women than men. In such cases, the choice for many women is between sharing a husband with full rights and having no family at all. In other cases a wife may be chronically ill or unable to have children, and both spouses prefer a second marriage to divorce.

Societies that ban polygamy have not ended the reality behind it. Men have secret relationships with women who then have no legal rights, no security and no recognition for their children. Islam's position is that if a man has a relationship with a second woman, he must marry her openly and bear the full responsibility of a husband and father.

## The Prophet's example

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was married to one woman, Khadija, for about twenty-five years, through all of his youth, and took no other wife while she lived. His later marriages, mostly to widows, came in the last years of his life, when he was building alliances and caring for the families of fallen Companions.

## In practice

Polygamy is permitted in Islam, and it is neither required nor encouraged. The overwhelming majority of Muslim men today, as in the past, have one wife. Jamal Badawi discusses the subject carefully in *Gender Equity in Islam*.
