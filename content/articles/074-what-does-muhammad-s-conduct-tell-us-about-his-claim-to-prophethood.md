Start with the eclipse on the day Muhammad’s son Ibrahim died. A report says people linked the two events. Muhammad rejected that explanation and encouraged prayer, seeking forgiveness and charity. Al-Sayyid sees this as a case where Muhammad declined an opportunity to exploit a coincidence.

The book places this alongside three other examples. In reports of his appeal at Mount Safa and Heraclius’s questioning of Abu Sufyan, people who knew him attest to his reputation for honesty. In Aisha’s account of a public accusation against her, Muhammad consults others and waits before reciting the revelation that clears her. The Qur’an also preserves rebukes or corrections addressed to Muhammad, including at the opening of chapter 80.

Character raises questions about motives and credibility. It is possible for a sincere person to be mistaken, so the book puts these reports beside evidence from the Qur’an, the teachings he conveyed and reported events. Al-Ghazali and Ibn Taymiyya also discuss several kinds of evidence, whose weight can vary from reader to reader.
